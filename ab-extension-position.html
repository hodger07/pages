Executive Read

The other side tried to compress the whole fight into “one last issue.” Rob turned that against them: fine — if the payable is the last issue, prove it. If they can’t, waive it or decouple it from the extension. That is the strongest frame right now.

What Rob’s Email Does

  • Creates a record of repeated requests: March 4, March 13, April 17, and direct requests before counsel got involved.
  • Refuses to accept their “last issue” framing unless the issue is actually supported.
  • Shifts burden back where it belongs: you want money, show invoices / ledger / basis.
  • Separates willingness to resolve the extension from willingness to accept unsupported debt consequences.
  • Gives Lathrop a clean off-ramp: produce it, waive it, or decouple it.

How Lathrop Receives It

They know this is a problem. Sturm’s “send other comments so we know if this is the last issue” was not strength. It was controlled narrowing. He is trying to move the file while avoiding a wider record.

Likely internal message to AB

“If you want to keep pressing this receivable, we need backup. If you don’t have backup, do not let this become the thing that blows up the extension.”

The Fuckup: They Tied the Payable Into the Extension

That was the mistake. If they had kept the old receivable separate, it would remain boring accounting noise. By embedding it in the extension/default framework, they made it central.

Release + alleged old payable + default/termination mechanics = “sign the release, then risk termination pressure over unsupported numbers.”

That may or may not have been the intent, but it is absolutely the appearance. And in a dispute like this, appearance matters because it becomes the story a judge, mediator, or business principal can understand in ten seconds.

Rich / Albert Leverage

  • The payable has not been substantiated after repeated requests.
  • The amount has changed materially over time.
  • The alleged debt is old, making documentation and collectability weaker.
  • They created bad optics by linking the receivable to default/termination risk.
  • Rich’s side is not blocking the extension — it is blocking unsupported default consequences.
  • Waiver or decoupling looks commercially reasonable.

Authority Brands Leverage

  • They control the draft paper and can slow-walk.
  • They still have brand/contract pressure tools.
  • If they produce credible backup, they regain leverage on amount, timing, offsets, or payment structure.
  • They can still try to frame Rich as holding up the deal over one issue.

Net: on this specific payable issue, Rich has the better position unless they produce clean support.

Why Lathrop May Be Pressing AB Internally

Probably yes — but not in dramatic terms. More likely they are saying: this is a bad leverage point unless you can document it. Lathrop does not need to believe AB is wrong on everything to know this payable/default linkage is messy.

Probable Lathrop advice: “You cannot keep demanding money without backup.” “You should not tie a disputed receivable into an extension if the stated goal is renewal/extension.” “Decide whether you want a clean extension or you want to preserve stale AR leverage.”

The Three Likely Responses

1

They produce backup

Then the fight moves to amount, offsets, timing, and whether the debt belongs anywhere near default mechanics. Proof of some amount does not equal default linkage.

2

They push without backup

That confirms weakness. The response stays narrow: unsupported amounts cannot be extension conditions, release leverage, default triggers, or termination tools.

3

They waive or decouple

That is the clean business resolution. Waiver is best. Decoupling works only if it fully removes the payable from extension/default/termination mechanics.

Recommended Posture Now

Hold the line: produce, waive, or decouple.

Do not send a sprawling redline yet. Do not accept the “last issue” frame unless the payable is solved cleanly. The strongest posture is: we are ready to move forward, but not with unsupported numbers embedded in termination machinery.

  • If they have support, produce it.
  • If they cannot support it, waive it.
  • If they insist on preserving it, decouple it completely from the extension, release, default, and termination path.
  • Do not concede that substantiation alone means it belongs inside the extension.

  • Do not accept “last issue” unless the payable is cleanly resolved.

Bottom Line

They stepped on the rake. By tying an old, shifting, unsupported receivable to the extension/default structure, they turned a weak accounting issue into a clean negotiation leverage point for Rich and Albert. Calm pressure wins: prove it, waive it, or get it out of the extension.
