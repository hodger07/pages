The Spike Story — What Happened in 2024?

📈 Cancel Rates Nearly Doubled in Katy & Spring

The Magnitude: Between 2023 and 2024, Katy's cancel rate surged from 12.4% to 22.3% (+9.9pp) and Spring jumped from 13.5% to 22.5% (+9.0pp). In absolute terms, Katy added 709 additional cancellations and Spring added 335 — on top of volume growth of ~32%. The system was getting busier and leakier at the same time.
Timing: The spike was not gradual. It concentrated in summer 2024. Katy hit 34.0% in July. Spring hit 33.8% in July. Combined, July 2024 posted a 31.3% cancel rate — nearly 1-in-3 booked jobs walking. Q1 2024 was actually normal (12–17% range), meaning whatever drove this hit hard and fast around May–July.
The Reason Shift: In 2023, the top cancel reasons were diffuse and operational (unlisted/other, customer fixed issue, changed mind). In 2024, "Changed Mind" exploded — becoming the #1 reason with 560 combined instances (up from 206 in 2023). This suggests a demand generation or booking quality problem, not a dispatch or service problem. Customers who weren't ready were being booked.

⚠️ DFW Warning — The Territory Still Moving the Wrong Direction

🚨 DFW Is the Outlier — Worst Cancel Rate & Still Climbing

The Problem: While Katy and Spring improved in 2025, DFW went the other way. Cancel rate rose from 20.3% (2024) to 23.4% (2025) — a +3.1pp deterioration. DFW is now the highest-cancel territory and has never been below 20% in the 3-year period.
Volume Tells the Story: DFW is the only territory that grew volume in both 2024 (+18.5%) and 2025 (+12.5%). They're adding jobs but not fixing the cancel problem. That's a quality-over-quantity warning: more bookings at a broken close rate means more waste, not more revenue.
November–December 2025 Emergency: DFW posted 32.9% in November and 28.6% in December 2025. That's a Q4 collapse. Combined with July 2025 at 28.7%, there's a pattern of seasonal or structural failure in DFW that isn't resolving on its own.

2025 Recovery — What Changed in Katy & Spring?

✅ Real Improvement — but Still Well Above 2023 Levels

The Good News: Katy dropped 2.0pp (22.3%→20.3%) and Spring dropped 2.6pp (22.5%→19.9%) in 2025. Both territories also saw absolute cancel counts fall — Katy by 132 fewer cancels, Spring by 77 fewer — while jobs stayed roughly flat. That's genuine operational improvement.
What Changed in the Reasons: The big story is the collapse of "Changed Mind" — Katy fell from 308 to 53, Spring from 169 to 25. Whatever booking or confirmation process tightened in 2025, it worked. In its place, "Service Not Offered" emerged (Katy 96, Spring 36) — suggesting scope clarity issues that need addressing.
What Didn't Change: Summer still spikes. Katy hit 28.5% in July 2025. Spring hit 30.8% in July 2025. The seasonal pattern is persistent. The improvement happened in Q1 and Q4, not summer — which means the intervention hasn't solved the volume surge problem.

Action Plan — Data-Grounded Recommendations

📋 What to Do

1
Intervene in DFW Immediately — It's a Different Problem Than Katy/Spring
DFW's top 2025 cancel drivers are "No Confirmation on ETA" (39), "Found Another Company Sooner" (54), and "Schedule Conflict" (58) — these are dispatch and communication failures, not booking quality failures. Deploy same-day confirmation texts and tighten the technician dispatch window from the moment of booking. DFW's Nov 2025 (32.9%) and Dec 2025 (28.6%) are Q4 emergencies that suggest the problem is accelerating, not stabilizing.
2
Address "Service Not Offered" Before It Becomes the New #1 Reason
In 2025, Katy logged 96 "Service Not Offered" cancellations and Spring logged 36 — a reason that barely appeared in prior years. This is a scope mismatch at booking: either CSRs are booking jobs outside the field team's capabilities, or the service catalog changed without updating the booking intake. Audit the booking funnel for service-type mismatches. Every "Service Not Offered" cancel is 100% preventable at the front end.
3
Build a Summer Surge Playbook — The Seasonal Spike Is Structural
July is the worst month across all three territories in all three years. 2024 Combined hit 31.3%. 2025 Combined hit 29.0%. This isn't noise — it's a predictable pattern driven by summer demand volume and longer scheduling windows. Solutions: aggressive confirmation outreach in May–August, shorter booking windows (48–72 hrs max) during peak season, and a dedicated "hot weather" cancel-rescue team that makes same-day saves when cancellations come in.
4
Double Down on What Fixed "Changed Mind" in Katy — Then Export It
Katy's "Changed Mind" cancels dropped from 308 (2024) to 53 (2025) — an 83% reduction. Something changed in the booking confirmation or pre-appointment process. Identify exactly what that was (appointment reminder protocol, booking script change, confirmation call timing), document it, and apply it in DFW — where "Changed Mind" is still 36 cancels and the behavioral pattern is less controlled.
5
Target High-Cancel ZIPs with Retention-Focused Outreach
ZIP 75035 (DFW) ran 35.6% cancels in 2025 on 59 jobs. ZIP 75093 hit 34.2%. ZIP 77346 (Spring) hit 25.6%. These aren't random — they're geographic clusters that likely share competitor density, demographic, or routing factors. Map cancellation heatmaps by ZIP, deploy competitive pricing research in the top 5 cancel ZIPs per territory, and test a "price match or beat" offer specifically in those codes. The volume is concentrated enough to move the needle.
