🚨 The Three Most Dangerous Clauses

1
HIGHEST RISK · Para 8a

The $3,000 Seller Default Cap — Asymmetric Remedies

If Chesmar walks — fails to build, terminates, or defaults — Kelsey's maximum recovery is $3,000 total ($2,500 deposit + $500). No specific performance. No consequential damages. No recovery for actual losses.

What actually goes wrong: Chesmar hits financial stress in 2026. Rates spike, material costs blow out. It's economically rational for them to cancel and relist at market. Cost to cancel Kelsey's deal: $3,000. By that point she may have sold/exited her current lease, lost other homes she turned down, paid for appraisals/inspections, and locked a rate now moved against her. None of that is recoverable. The lis pendens waiver (Para 8d) means she can't even cloud title to create leverage.

Industry benchmark: Acceptable builder default remedy = full refund of all deposits + documented out-of-pocket costs + $10K-$15K liquidated damages. Or specific performance if substantially complete.

2
HIGH RISK · Para 12

Mandatory Arbitration — Binds Heirs & Runs With the Deed

All disputes → binding arbitration. Jury trial waived. This clause transfers with the deed and binds heirs, successors, and future buyers.

Scenario A — Defect: Six months after closing, foundation issues emerge (common in Harris County's expansive clay soils). Chesmar points to the 2-10 warranty insurer; insurer disputes coverage. Kelsey is stuck in arbitration against Chesmar, fighting the warranty insurer separately, with no jury option and arbitration costs that may exceed smaller claim values.
Scenario B — Resale: Kelsey sells in 3-4 years. Her buyer's lender discovers the arbitration clause runs with the deed — this is unusual, may require disclosure, and can complicate title insurance and financing.
3
HIGH RISK · Para 16 + 14c

Force Majeure Termination + Silent Material Substitution

Para 16 lets Chesmar terminate and refund all money if delayed 30+ days by force majeure — including "unavailability of materials at current prices." Para 14c lets them substitute materials without notice ("comparable quality" — defined by Chesmar).

What actually goes wrong: This is the builder's built-in escape valve. If lumber, HVAC components, or appliances spike in cost, Chesmar can: (A) quietly substitute cheaper materials under Para 14c with no notice, or (B) terminate entirely under Para 16. The "at current prices" language is exceptionally broad — almost any material could be argued unavailable "at current prices" during inflation. Kelsey could close on a home with materially different finishes than she selected, with no contractual recourse.

🗺️ Unincorporated Harris County — 3 Specific Risks

1. Building Inspection & "Substantial Completion" Standard

Cypress/Bridgeland is in unincorporated Harris County — no city building department. The contract says substantial completion = "any government-sanctioned inspector." In practice, this means a county-level or third-party inspector, not the more rigorous city inspection process used in Houston proper or Sugar Land. The practical risk: the closing trigger is easier to satisfy, potentially forcing Kelsey to close on a home that isn't truly finished to a consumer standard. This is especially relevant for punch-list items and systems testing.

3. Flood Exposure — Harris County History

Bridgeland/Cypress area of Harris County has historically experienced significant flooding (Harvey 2017, Imelda 2019, and others). Unincorporated areas have less strict drainage requirements than incorporated cities. The contract provides no flood representations (Para 25 explicitly disclaims any). Buyer should: (1) pull the FEMA flood zone map for 12751 Grayson County Dr., (2) obtain independent flood elevation certificate, (3) budget for flood insurance even if not required by lender — National Flood Insurance Program rates for Harris County have increased significantly post-Harvey.

📝 Recommended Contract Modifications — Ranked by Priority

Priority 1 Para 8a — Seller Default Remedies

Increase seller default remedy from $3,000 to meaningful compensation

CURRENT: "Buyer's sole remedy = return of $2,500 + lesser of $2,500 or $500"
PROPOSED: "In the event of Seller's material default, Buyer shall be entitled to: (i) return of all deposits, option amounts, and change-order payments paid by Buyer; (ii) reimbursement of documented out-of-pocket costs including but not limited to lender fees, inspection fees, appraisal costs, and title-related expenses; and (iii) liquidated damages of $10,000 as reasonable compensation for lost opportunity. Alternatively, if the Improvements are substantially complete, Buyer may seek specific performance."

Chesmar will likely refuse specific performance. Focus on full refund + documented costs + $10K floor. They may agree to the cost reimbursement piece even if they won't add liquated damages.

Priority 2 Para 4a — Closing Notice Period

Extend 3-day notice to 7 business days minimum

CURRENT: "Closing and funding will occur within three (3) days following PURCHASER'S final Loan approval and/or clear to close"
PROPOSED: "Closing and funding will occur within seven (7) business days following PURCHASER'S receipt of written notice of substantial completion and final Loan approval and/or clear to close, whichever is later."

This is highly negotiable. Chesmar controls when they send the notice — they never give only 3 days in practice. This just formalizes what actually happens.

Priority 3 Para 16 — Narrow Force Majeure Language

Remove "unavailability of materials at current prices" as force majeure trigger

CURRENT: "Force majeure includes...unavailability of materials at current prices..."
PROPOSED: Remove "unavailability of materials at current prices" from the force majeure definition. Force majeure should be limited to events outside Seller's reasonable control that make completion physically impossible, not economically inconvenient. Add: "Economic conditions, cost increases, or material price changes do not constitute force majeure events under this Contract."

This closes the built-in profit-protection escape valve. Chesmar may resist but this clause is genuinely overreaching.

Priority 4 Para 14c — Material Substitution Notice

Require advance written notice before any material substitution

CURRENT: "Seller may substitute materials of comparable quality, utility, or color without notice"
PROPOSED: "Seller shall provide Buyer with written notice at least five (5) business days prior to making any material substitution. Buyer shall have the right to approve or reject any proposed substitution within three (3) business days of receipt of notice. If Buyer does not respond within three (3) business days, the substitution shall be deemed approved."

Operationally easy for Chesmar. Protects Kelsey from discovering substitutions only at walk-through.

Priority 5 Para 4a — Third-Party Inspector Right

Add explicit right to private inspection before closing

CURRENT: Contract does not explicitly address buyer's right to third-party inspector
PROPOSED: Add: "Buyer shall have the right to conduct, at Buyer's expense, one (1) independent third-party inspection of the Improvements within five (5) business days prior to the scheduled Closing date. Seller shall provide reasonable access for such inspection during normal business hours. Findings from said inspection that were not previously disclosed shall be added to the walk-through deficiency list."

Chesmar may allow this in practice already but having it in writing is critical.

Priority 6 Para 4a — Walk-Through Deficiency Repair Timeline

Add repair completion commitment to walk-through deficiency list

CURRENT: "PURCHASER agrees to a new home orientation... to develop a list of any deficiencies or adjustments which need to be made" (no repair timeline)
PROPOSED: Add: "Seller agrees to complete all items on the walk-through deficiency list within thirty (30) days following Closing, or such other date as mutually agreed in writing. Seller shall provide Buyer with written confirmation of completion. Failure to complete deficiency list items within said period shall entitle Buyer to seek repair at Seller's expense up to $2,500 through the warranty process."

This makes "noted on the list" actually mean something post-closing.

Priority 7 Side Letter — Completion Date Commitment

Request side letter acknowledging July 2026 target and Para 16 notice obligations

CURRENT: "Estimated Completion: July" — not contractually binding
PROPOSED SIDE LETTER: "Chesmar Homes acknowledges that the estimated completion date for the above-referenced home is on or before [specific date in July 2026]. In the event of delays anticipated to extend beyond [date + 30 days], Chesmar agrees to provide Buyer with written notice within five (5) business days of identifying such delay. This letter does not modify the Contract but reflects Seller's good-faith completion commitment."

Not a contract modification — just a good-faith letter. Chesmar may agree because it's non-binding. Gives Kelsey warning before triggering rate lock or moving decisions.
