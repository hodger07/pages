The Play — How Date 4 Should Go

The Rule: Share, Don't Probe. Kasey leads with his truth. He doesn't interview her. Vulnerability invites vulnerability — she's already started (custody on Date 3). He matches her depth with his own. She's in the pool. He just needs to get in with her.

Phase 1 — The Kitchen (first 60-90 min)

  • Cook together. Keep it light. Music on. Let the domestic energy do its thing — two people building something side by side is its own kind of intimacy.
  • Physical: touch her naturally while cooking. Hand on the small of her back. Taste something and offer it to her. Don't force it — let the proximity do the work.
  • This phase builds safety. She needs to feel settled before the real conversation happens.

Phase 2 — The Transition (after dinner, move to couch/patio)

  • The conversation will shift naturally. Don't force a topic change. Let dinner end, let the pace slow down, let the silence arrive.
  • If it doesn't shift naturally, one line opens the door: "I've been thinking about something you said last time — about your dad having custody." Then stop. Let her take it wherever she wants.

Phase 3 — The Real Conversation

Kasey's talking points — in this order:

1. Lead with his truth first.
"My stepmom Jessica has ALS. It's terminal. I lost my brother Michael. And after Michael, my niece Paige died too. Those losses changed everything about how I see relationships. I'm not interested in surface anymore. I need someone who can sit with the hard stuff."

Don't elaborate. Don't explain. Say the true thing and stop talking.

2. Watch her response.
What good looks like: Silence. Eye contact. Maybe tears. "Tell me about Michael." or "Tell me about Paige." She moves toward the pain, not away from it.
What also may be good: Because she's training as a PA, she may instinctively shift into triage, care, or problem-solving language. That is not automatically a miss. It's her wiring. If she does, Kasey should let her care without bristling, then softly steer if needed: "You don't have to fix it. Just being here with me is enough."
What bad looks like: "Oh my God, I'm so sorry" + immediate subject change, or detached clinical management with no feeling underneath it. She manages it instead of sitting in it.
What great looks like: She shares something of her own without being asked. That's the matching move. Or she adjusts in real time when he tells her he needs presence, not solutions.

3. If she matches — then go to her story.
Not as interrogation. As genuine curiosity from someone who just showed his cards.
"What was that like — growing up with your dad? Like, who was there for the stuff a mom usually handles?"

4. Listen for process vs. package.
Processed: She can talk about it with emotion AND perspective. She's felt the pain and come through it. She might tear up. She might say "I don't know" to some of it. That's real.
Packaged: She has a clean narrative. "It was fine, I turned out okay, my dad was great." No emotion in the telling. That's a script, not a wound that's been tended to.

5. The grief question — don't ask it directly.
Don't say "can you handle my grief." Instead: "When the hard stuff hits — and it will — I go somewhere for a while. I need someone who doesn't take that personally. Does that make sense to you?"
Her answer tells him everything. Either she's been there herself and gets it — or she hasn't.

Neither is wrong. This is not a story about physical disloyalty. It's a story about whether she can tolerate his emotional weather without making it mean he left.

This is not a dealbreaker. It's a "know what you're walking into." In fact, Kasey's last girlfriend could not tolerate this exact pattern. That makes it real, not theoretical. Date 4 is still too early for the full conversation — but by date 8-10 they need language around it: "I don't leave. But when grief hits me, I can get quiet and hard to reach. It's not rejection. Physical Escalation — Guidance

Both models agree: do not sleep together tonight.

The depth conversation needs to happen on its own terms. If they have the hard conversation AND have sex on the same night, the emotional rawness gets conflated with physical intimacy — and neither person knows which one created the closeness.

What's appropriate: Make out. Escalate from Date 3. But he pulls back before it goes all the way. Same frame as before: "I want to do this right with you."

Key: After the emotional conversation, physical intimacy will feel magnetic. That's biology — vulnerability triggers bonding hormones. That's exactly why he should hold the line. Let the emotional bond set before adding sex to it. She'll respect it. And if she's the right person, it'll make her want him more.

After Date 4 — The Trip

  • She leaves town. Absence after a deep date + physical escalation is the highest-interest-generating sequence possible.
  • Communication during trip: Call her once every 1-2 days. Not text. She told him she prefers voice. Keep calls short — 10-15 minutes. "Just wanted to hear your voice before you headed out." That's it.
  • Do NOT: Double-text. Express anxiety about the distance. Over-communicate. Let her miss him.
  • Watch for: Does she initiate calls? Does she reference the emotional conversation from Date 4? Does she make plans for when she's back? All green signals.
  • If she goes quiet during the trip: Don't panic. Remember — she's a self-described horrible texter AND she has avoidant tendencies. One call attempt, one voicemail. If she calls back within 24 hours, it's fine. If she goes dark for 3+ days after the depth of Date 4 — that's avoidant attachment activating. Data, not disaster.
