Demographic Context (why the Collin Co. corridor still matters)

The Collin County batch list (Cluster D) includes 75002 Allen, 75013 Frisco, 75070/75071 McKinney, 75078 Prosper. These are the strongest-growth ZIPs in the country right now and we want to keep showing up — just on our toll-rationalized terms.

  • Prosper 75078 ranks 9th fastest-growing ZIP in the U.S. — population +254%, housing +243% over the past decade.
  • North Fort Worth 76177 is the 7th fastest in the U.S. — and it sits in our Cluster A, so we already win on it.
  • DFW metro added 123,557 residents last year (~339/day). 4th-largest metro in the U.S.
  • Denton County remains a top-5 growth county nationally — Jamieson's home base is in the right place.
  • Keller (76248) has plateaued (−2.6% 2019–2023). Mature, dense, ideal dispatch hub — but the growth is east (Collin) and north (Denton corridor), which is why batching them right is essential.

Assumptions & How to Tighten

  • Toll cost classes are conservative DFW averages (TollTag rates). Replace with your actual T-Tag invoice and the number sharpens.
  • Drive savings model assumes ~3 stops/batch day in Cluster D and ~2 stops/batch in Cluster C. Higher density = bigger savings.
  • Revenue math uses your numbers: $2,000 average ticket × 70% conversion rate, with 2 hours per dispatch (drive + onsite). Each input is independently tunable — every 5-point conversion-rate move shifts the annual incremental by ~$34K; every $250 of ticket size shifts it by ~$60K.
  • Baseline assumes each job is a round trip from the nearest anchor. Real-world consecutive-job routing already saves some — so the recapture is incremental on top of what you're doing today.
