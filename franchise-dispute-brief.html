The Renewal Addendum Trap

Both the December 2025 and February 2026 Renewal Addenda are structurally designed to weaponize these disputed balances:

Section 2 "Receivables": Embeds all disputed balances as conditions of signing. Bustos $149,761.16, Incredible $18,328.21, RGRH $4,513.35 — all must be "brought current at time of signing."
5-Business-Day Forfeiture Window: Any dispute must be submitted within 5 business days or franchisee "will no longer have the opportunity to bring any additional requests for review." Engineered to force signature before reconciliation is possible.
Exhibit C — General Release: Signing requires releasing AB "from any and all suits, claims... from the beginning of time to the Effective Date, known or unknown" — including the ServiceTitan/CFAA claims and bad-faith-billing claims.
The Trap Sequence: Sign → release ALL claims (Exhibit C) → dispute embedded amounts → AB declares default on unpaid "receivables" → AB terminates all 14 franchise agreements + triggers non-compete.

Bustos Investments — stated $149,761.16.

This figure cannot be reconciled with Authority Brands' own records, and is the most serious of the four.

The demand is driven principally by Invoice INVMSPRK-29403 ($88,044.16, dated 12/31/2024) — a retroactive backbill for 66 months at $3,750/month reaching to approximately mid-2019. Three independent problems require resolution before this invoice can be treated as valid. First, while the signed Amendment (effective August 2018) does establish a $3,750/month minimum for the Territory Group, that minimum was to be captured through AB's normal semi-monthly billing cycle — this invoice represents a retroactive backbill never invoiced through that cycle, booked as a manual journal entry on 12/31/2024, sixteen months after AB's own legal counsel fixed the Bustos balance at $112,918.96 with no such charge included. Second, the charge reaches to mid-2019; amounts predating June 2022 are barred by the applicable four-year limitations period and cannot be collected regardless of their merit. Third, this invoice appears in neither Lani Binnie's August 2023 reconciliation (which fixed the Bustos balance at $112,918.96) nor in Van Nguyen's contemporaneous capture-billing records — raising the question of when and on what basis it was created. Fourth, the 66-month period covered by INVMSPRK-29403 includes all months after Q3–Q4 2020, when MS0156 (South Denton) was shut down due to failure and RGRH (MS0110) assumed coverage of that territory and paid royalties to Authority Brands on that revenue — an arrangement documented in writing and accepted by Authority Brands without protest. Charging minimum royalties against a closed, non-operating franchise for those same periods, while simultaneously collecting royalties on the identical territory's revenue through RGRH, constitutes a direct double collection. We hold written documentation from Q3–Q4 2020 confirming this arrangement and will produce it on request.

Most significantly: on Credit Memo CMMSPRK-332 (2/28/2025), Authority Brands wrote off $97,738.84 against this same MS0135 account, expressly designated "Bad Debt Write Off / Cleanup AR 2024," and fully applied. Authority Brands cannot write a balance off as uncollectible and then, fifteen months later, demand $149,761.16 on the same account. We ask that Authority Brands explain the relationship between the February 2025 write-off and the present demand.
