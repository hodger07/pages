v1 put AI in M3 boxes. v2 makes AI the central nervous system. Every human interaction — every call, every tech visit, every review — is captured, analyzed, and fed back into optimization. This is the core product differentiation from ServiceTitan.

Every call recorded via Dialpad integration
Real-time transcription + Own LLM coaching for CSRs during calls

MIDDLE Tech Onsite AI

Every tech visit recorded via Whisper/LLM on tech mobile app

Financial optimization: job profitability by type/tech/zone → pricing & staffing recommendations

Architecture: Challenge Everything

v1 assumed Laravel monolith + React Native. That was the safe answer. For an AI-heavy platform with recording pipelines, real-time coaching, and continuous analysis, we need to question that assumption.

The Core Question: Monolith or Services?

ApproachProsCons
Laravel Monolith (current)Existing code, team knows it, fast CRUD, single deployAI/ML pipelines don't fit PHP. Recording analysis needs Python/Go. Scaling AI independently = impossible.
Full MicroservicesIndependent scaling, right-tool-per-jobMassive ops overhead for a 7-person team. Distributed debugging hell. Over-engineered for year 1.
Modular Monolith + AI SidecarLaravel handles CRUD/business logic. Python/FastAPI sidecar handles all AI/ML. Event bus connects them.Two codebases to maintain. Need clear API contract.
🎯 Recommendation: Modular Monolith + AI Sidecar

Keep Laravel for what it's good at: CRUD, auth, multi-tenant, business rules, API layer. Don't rewrite 88K LOC.

Add a Python/FastAPI AI service for everything ML: transcription processing, sentiment analysis, coaching recommendations, predictive pricing, performance scoring. Python owns the AI/ML ecosystem — fighting that in PHP is swimming upstream.

Connect them with an event bus (Redis Streams or NATS — not Kafka, too heavy for this team size). Events: call.recorded, visit.completed, review.received, estimate.created. The AI service subscribes, processes, publishes results back.

Mobile: Stay React Native but consider Expo for faster iteration. Flutter is better for offline but the team knows RN and jfi-mobile is 35K LOC. Switching frameworks = 3-month tax. Not worth it. Invest that time in a solid offline sync layer (WatermelonDB or PowerSync).

Tradeoff accepted: Two languages (PHP + Python) means broader hiring needs. Mitigated by: AI sidecar is a focused service with clear boundaries, and AI/ML engineers expect Python anyway.

What the Optimization Engine Actually Does

In plain English: It watches every number in your business — from QuickBooks, from calls, from reviews, from job data — and tells you what to fix before you know it's broken.

  • "Tech A did $48K, Tech B did $31K — why?" → Engine correlates call scores: A explains well (8.2), B rushes (5.1). Auto-generates coaching plan for B.
  • "Water heater installs: 42% margin in Katy, 28% in Spring — why?" → Engine finds Spring pricing is $400 below market. Pricebook auto-suggests adjustment.
  • "Customer retention dropped 12% in Q4 — why?" → Callback rate spiked on Friday PM jobs. Techs rushing to finish the week. Recommendation: no installs Friday PM, or add QA gate.
  • "You'll need 2 more techs by June" → Lead volume up 15%, techs at 92% utilization. Historical pattern: capacity breaks at 95%. Hire now or lose revenue in 60 days.

Revenue Forecasting: 30/60/90 day projections with confidence ranges. "March: $1.2M ± $80K. Trending 8% below last March — here's why: 2 fewer techs, 15% fewer leads from this zip code."

All from data you already generate. QB has the money. Calls have the behavior. Reviews have the outcomes. The engine just connects the dots.

Day 1 Without ST Feature Set: Book a call → create a customer → schedule a job → dispatch a tech → tech executes with mobile app (offline) → generate invoice → sync to QuickBooks → get paid → AI scores every interaction → pricebook auto-adjusts → revenue forecast updates. That's the heartbeat. Your Moneyball for HVAC.

Honest Assessment: 42% is aggressive but defensible for a CRUD-heavy platform where AI excels (forms, resolvers, tests, components). Hard Truth: You don't build a custom platform to save on SaaS fees. You build it because ServiceTitan can't do what the AI spine does — the continuous feedback loop from call to review to optimization. That's the value prop. If the AI coaching and optimization features don't materially improve revenue and margins, the ROI math doesn't work. The bet is on the intelligence layer, not the CRUD replacement.
