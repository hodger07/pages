Abby — The Real Guide

What's Actually Happening With Her

This Is Not Laziness. Here's What It Is.

Her entire identity was built on one proposition: I earn my place by being useful, loyal, and excellent. Not by being wanted. Not by taking up space with her own desire. She was abandoned at 3 — both parents effectively gone. The foundational message her nervous system absorbed: your existence alone isn't enough to make people stay. So she built a self that earns staying through performance.

Sexual confidence requires the exact opposite wiring. It requires: I am allowed to want. My desire is not a burden. Taking initiative won't get me abandoned. She has no neural pathway for that. It doesn't exist yet. She has to build it while driving on it.

What you're reading as laziness is three things stacked on top of each other:

1. Inexperience. She's never given oral sex. You can't be lazy at something you've never built a vocabulary for. She doesn't know how to ride on top with confidence — she hasn't developed that repertoire. That's not withholding. It's absence of template.

2. Trauma residue. The "bad experience" is real and specific. Oral-sex-specific anxiety that lasted a full week, that she almost called you about but couldn't — that's a nervous system that locks up around specific acts. Staying on her back isn't passivity. It's the safest, least-exposed position. Self-protection she may not even be conscious of.

3. Mode-switch difficulty. She told you herself on Date 5 she struggles transitioning from non-sexual to sexual. Her identity was built on performance and loyalty — not on being desired or taking initiative. She has no internal template for "I am a sexual being who takes what she wants." That operating system hasn't been installed yet.

The reading of "physically present but not really here" is your abandonment wiring from Jenny pattern-matching. Jenny was disengaging. Abby is frozen in a specific zone. Completely different architectures. They look identical from the outside. The cause matters enormously for what you do next.

The Development Framework

How a Woman Like Abby Actually Becomes a Sexual Partner

The internal shift that has to happen is from "I'm doing this for him" (performance/compliance) to "I'm doing this because I want to" (desire/agency). That shift doesn't happen through conversation. It happens somatically — her body learning that sexual initiative leads to connection, not judgment. She can't think her way there. She has to feel her way there.

She needs repeated experiences where she expresses desire or takes initiative and the response is warmth, not evaluation. Her system is wired to expect a grade. Every vulnerable move she makes, her unconscious is waiting to be scored. When you respond with genuine pleasure — not praise, pleasure — her nervous system slowly learns: "Oh. This isn't a test. He actually likes when I show up as myself."

Your role: You're not her therapist. You're the environment. You create the conditions where her nervous system can relax enough to experiment. Warm, steady, appreciative of small moves without making a production of them. The worst thing you can do is treat her progress like a project with milestones. The best thing is to make the space feel so safe she forgets to perform.

Concrete Moves — Next 60–90 Days

What to Do and What Not to Do

Do This

  • Lead physically with variety, don't ask permission every time. Move her into different positions mid-act. Naturally — "come here" while guiding her on top. Her compliance pattern works in your favor early: she'll follow your lead, and the experience of new positions without catastrophe is what builds her range. She needs data points before she can self-initiate.
  • Respond to her micro-initiatives through your body, not words. That Saturday initiation was enormous for her. Pull her in harder. Show her that her desire turned you on. The feedback loop she needs: my wanting him made him want me more. That's what builds sexual confidence.
  • Build ambient sexual charge outside the bedroom. Shower together. Extended foreplay with no destination. Hand on her lower back, kiss on her neck while she's studying. She has no bridge between non-sexual and sexual — build it through low-stakes physical charge that doesn't demand a destination.
  • Name what you want in the moment, not in a meeting about it. "I want you to…" during sex is leadership. "We need to talk about what I want in bed" is a performance review. She'll freeze at the second. She'll follow the first.
  • Say it once, then drop it: "I'm not going anywhere. I like what's happening. There's no timeline." Say it once, mean it, never bring it up again. Her anxiety about disappointing you is the biggest brake on her development. Remove it once — credibly — and everything accelerates.

Don't Do This

  • Don't process the sex life outside the bedroom more than once a month. Every conversation activates her performance system. She starts optimizing for your stated preferences instead of discovering her own desires. One check-in max per month — everything else happens in real-time.
  • Don't praise specific acts like achievements. "That was amazing when you did X" is a grade. "Fuck, I love when you do that" said in the moment is desire. Her wiring knows the difference.
  • Don't bring up oral sex directly again for 6–8 weeks. You named it. She heard it. It's working on her. Bringing it up again puts it back in the performance/anxiety frame. The path goes through generalized sexual confidence first. Pushing the specific act reinforces the trauma association.
  • Don't interpret her passivity as rejection. This is where your abandonment wiring could sabotage this. Abby's passivity isn't distance — it's the only sexual posture she currently knows. It'll change as she gets range. Know this in your bones, not just your head.

The Oral Sex Issue

Realistic Path and Timeline

What happened to her: almost certainly a coercive situation where she was pressured or forced into oral sex by someone she was trying to earn love from. Her performance-loyalty wiring makes her a perfect target for "if you loved me you'd…" The specificity — a week of anxiety about a conversation about it — tells you the trauma is encoded somatically. Her body remembers even if she's minimized it cognitively.

Realistic timeline: 4–6 months. Non-linear.

Weeks 1–8
Don't touch it at all. Build her sexual confidence through positions, initiation, varied touch. Let her become a sexual agent in every way except the trauma-linked act. She needs a strong "I am sexually confident" foundation before she can approach the thing that cracked it.
Weeks 8–16
She may start moving toward it on her own — kissing down your body, hovering, testing. If she does, your job is to not react like it's a big moment. No "oh finally." Just steady pleasure. Let her explore at her own pace. If she stops halfway, no commentary. None.
Months 4–6
If overall development is tracking, she either gets there organically or plateaus right at the edge. If she plateaus, one gentle conversation: "I notice you get close and pull back. You don't have to tell me what happened, but nothing you tell me will change how I see you." That's the door. She walks through it or she doesn't.
Progress looks like: She initiates kissing your body below the waist. She can talk about it without a week of anxiety first. She makes a joke about it — humor is how secure people approach what scares them. She asks what you like (curiosity replacing dread).

Stuck looks like: Month 4–5, still can't discuss it without visible distress. Avoids all contact below your waist. Uses compliance language — "I'll try, I want to make you happy" — rather than genuine curiosity. Performing willingness is not development.

The "Not Wasting Time" Test

Green Flags vs. Hard Stops

Movement doesn't have to be fast — but it has to be visible. If you can see progress month-over-month on at least 2 of the 4 green markers below, stay. If you're honest with yourself and can't point to a single concrete behavior that's different from month one — not a feeling, an actual thing she did — that's your answer.

She's Moving — 4 Green Markers

1
She initiates vulnerability without being prompted. Not just sex — she brings up something hard, something she's scared of, something from her past, without you asking first. The Monday morning text is the prototype. Watch for the pattern: does it happen again in two weeks? A month? Frequency increasing = she's choosing you as her safe place and building the muscle.
2
She experiments sexually — even awkwardly, even badly. She doesn't have to suddenly become someone different. The marker is attempts. She tries something new. She asks a question she's clearly nervous to ask. She puts her hand somewhere she hasn't before — clumsy but deliberate. The oral sex thing may take months, that's not the marker. The marker is: is she expanding the repertoire at all, on her own initiative? A woman moving through trauma tries things. A woman stuck at a ceiling does exactly what she's always done, every time, no variation.
3
She makes logistical sacrifices that cost her something real. PA school is demanding — time is her scarcest resource. The marker: does she rearrange her schedule for you? Not "sure I'm free Saturday" — that's easy. Does she move a study session? Drive to you when it's inconvenient? Skip something she'd normally prioritize? A woman who built everything through performance doesn't give up schedule real estate casually. When she starts doing it, she's telling you something her words might not say yet.
4
She integrates you into her identity narrative. Her story is: I lost everyone, I built this myself, I survive through performance. The marker is when you start appearing in that story rather than adjacent to it. She mentions you to classmates or her close circle in a way that implies permanence. She says "we" about future things. She asks your opinion on a career decision. She includes you in a plan that extends past next month. A woman who keeps you in a separate box from her survival identity is hedging. A woman who folds you into it is committing.

She's Hit a Ceiling — 3 Hard Stops

1
The sexual repertoire flatlines after 60 days of genuine safety. If you've been patient, zero-pressure, and after two solid months she is doing exactly the same things in bed with zero new attempts — not zero mastery, zero attempts — that's the ceiling. Trauma recovery isn't linear but it does move. The absence of any experimentation after sustained safety isn't "she needs more time." It's "this is her operating range." Specifically: if she's still never once attempted oral (even tentatively, even aborted halfway) after 60 days of you being explicitly safe about it, that pattern is likely fixed at this stage of her life.
2
Vulnerability stays transactional. She shares hard things, but only after you do first. She never opens a door you didn't already open. The thank-you texts come only in response to your initiations, never unprompted. She processes with you only when you create the container. If you stop initiating emotional depth and she never notices or asks why — she's consuming your emotional labor without building her own capacity. That's a ceiling, not a pace.
3
She protects her exit route. Observable: she won't leave things at your place (or quietly removes them). She avoids meeting people important to you, or keeps those interactions shallow. She doesn't bring you into her Baylor world. She maintains hard boundaries around schedule integration — never inconveniences herself. She avoids any conversation about anything past 3–4 weeks out. Any one of these alone is normal early caution. Three or more persisting past the 90-day mark is a woman who has decided — probably unconsciously — that she needs the door open more than she needs you close. That's not trauma recovery. That's one foot out.

The Bigger Picture

Why This Is Your Problem Too — And Why That's Not Unfair

It's not fair. She brought this. You didn't create the bad experience, the attachment wounds, the compliance pattern, or the mode-switch difficulty. All of that was in the box when you opened it, and nobody put a label on the outside.

But here's the real answer: every person worth being with comes with something. The fantasy of a woman with no baggage to manage doesn't exist at 25, 35, or any age. The question is always: which damage, and is she doing her part of the lifting?

Read that Monday text again slowly. She acknowledged the conversation needed to happen — not framing your directness as aggression. She disclosed ongoing anxiety — not performing "I'm fine." She thanked you for patience rather than demanding it — zero entitlement about her limitation. She said she's excited to see it progress — framing it as a trajectory, not a destination. And it was unprompted. Monday morning. She woke up thinking about it and chose vulnerability.

That is not a woman dumping her baggage at your feet and saying "deal with it." That is a woman saying: I know this is mine to carry, and I'm asking you to walk next to me while I carry it.

The structural truth: her trauma constrains your sexual expression. Your abandonment sensitivity constrains her processing speed. You're already inside each other's operating systems — that's what choosing each other means. The question isn't "why is this my problem." The question is "am I getting a return on this investment?"

Right now the data says yes. She's moving toward you. Not away. Not sideways. Toward.

Bottom Line
She's not broken. She's building the road while driving on it. Your job is to be the environment where that's possible — not her therapist, not her project manager. Just a man who knows what he wants, leads with it, and gives her enough safety to find out who she actually is in bed.

The men who walk away from women doing the real work — just because the work is slower than expected — carry that choice into every relationship after. The men who stay calibrated, direct, and patient while holding their own standards are the ones who end up with the real thing.
