Round 3: The Note What does it actually say?

Nadia set the ground rules: it must be about him making himself available, not about her being assessed. No commenting on her appearance. Alyssa required four elements: (1) identify himself non-threateningly, (2) be honest about what prompted it, (3) give contact info, (4) make it explicitly, genuinely okay not to respond. After extensive back-and-forth:

Hey —
I know this is forward, but I wanted to introduce myself. I'm Kasey Hodge, I live in the building.

If you ever want to grab coffee, here's my number: [xxx-xxx-xxxx]

No pressure — if not, just pretend this never happened, and I promise it won't be awkward.
— Kasey

OPUS VERDICT: DO IT

The vote is 8-1. The panel overwhelmingly endorsed the approach.

Here's what actually happened in this debate: nine women with very different threat models and romantic dispositions converged on the same answer. The note works because of who Kasey is — Assertiveness 23 means his natural communication style is exactly what women describe wanting but rarely encounter. He's not performing non-threatening. He IS non-threatening. The note reads that way because it's authentic.

The restraint is the MVP. The note is short, honest, and doesn't try too hard. No compliments on her appearance. No overexplaining. No gifts. Just a guy saying "I noticed you, here's my number, no pressure." In a world of DM slides and unsolicited pics, a handwritten notecard tucked under a windshield wiper is almost absurdly old-school — and that's exactly why it works.

The critical rule — and Kasey needs to hear this: If she doesn't text, it never happened. No second note. No "did you get my note?" No lingering eye contact in the garage. No asking mutual neighbors. He acts completely normal. The promise in the note has to be ironclad. That's what separates this from creepy. Taylor, the hardest no on the panel, said it herself: "If he actually means that — that's the whole ballgame."

What Kasey's Assertiveness 23 does here that a higher-assertiveness guy can't: A more aggressive guy writes this same note and it reads as calculated. Kasey writes it and it reads as genuine — because it IS. The soft, honest "I know this is forward" energy isn't a technique. It's him. And women can tell the difference.

📝 Execution Checklist

  • Buy a pack of clean cream or white notecards + small envelopes (Target, not Dollar Tree)
  • Handwrite in neat PRINT — not cursive, not all caps
  • Use the exact note above — don't overthink or rewrite
  • Tuck envelope under her windshield wiper (driver side)
  • Do it when the garage is empty — no audience, no pressure for either of them
  • Put his real phone number (not a Google Voice burner — she'll sense that)
  • Do NOT escalate if she doesn't respond — no second note, no asking around
  • Do NOT attach a gift (flowers, coffee card, anything)
  • Do NOT follow up if she doesn't respond — ever
  • Do NOT mention the note if he sees her in person
  • Do NOT ask the leasing office anything about her
  • Do NOT comment on her appearance in the note

💡 The Superpower Nobody Talks About

Kasey's Assertiveness 23 means he naturally gives easy outs, doesn't push, and reads as genuinely safe. In a world where women are exhausted by men who won't take no for an answer, a guy who structurally cannot be pushy is exactly the guy they hope left the note. He doesn't have to try to not be creepy. He just isn't.
