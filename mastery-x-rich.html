1 Which Archetype Are You?

Leonard describes four types: the Master, the Dabbler, the Obsessive, and the Hacker. You're not one of them. You're three of them in a trench coat, depending on the domain. Let's be honest about where each one shows up.

The Obsessive — Your Default Setting

This is home base. Physical Energy 97 Insistence 97 Thought 18 — you're wired for intense effort spikes, system building at breakneck speed, and gut-level decisions that skip the developmental timing Leonard says mastery requires. Leonard's Obsessive "doubles down forcefully" when the plateau appears. You said it yourself: "I speed up instead of slow down. The Undercover Agent disguises overwhelm as productivity." That's the Obsessive's signature move — mistaking acceleration for progress.

In business, this is the guy who spent $1M on software without asking hard questions. In the franchise, it was seven years of enduring instead of evolving. When the plateau arrived, you didn't love it — you tried to outrun it.

The Dabbler — In Relationships and Inner Work

This one's subtler, and you'll want to argue with it. The Dabbler loves beginnings — new energy, new identity, new context. You're walking away from a 17-year franchise. You're building a new platform. You're in deep coaching with Jon. You're doing ibogaine and 5-MeO. The pattern isn't the problem — sometimes you need to start over. The Dabbler question is: do you stay long enough for the plateau to teach you something, or do you interpret every plateau as a signal that it's the wrong fit?

Your Restlessness 52 / Need 17 is telling: moderate variety-seeking on the surface, but underneath you actually need stability. The Dabbler in you starts things. The need for stability means you suffer when the Dabbler runs the show. You feel the friction but misattribute it.

The Hacker — In Emotional Life

Leonard's Hacker reaches "good enough" and camps. Emotional Energy 23 / Need 51 — you present as stoic and detached, but you need emotional expression. You've hacked emotional life: functional enough to get by, never enough to fully arrive. You carry grief solo. You override body signals. You said, "I don't notice overwhelm until it's already running." That's not an awareness problem — that's a man who stopped developing emotional fluency at "good enough" because the real work of going deeper felt like weakness to a Self-Consciousness 1 who learned early that nobody's coming to help.

The Master — In Sobriety

Here's the thing you don't see. Forty years of sobriety is mastery. Daily inventory. Surrendering ego. Showing up when the plateau is boring and endless and nothing dramatic is happening. You've been on Leonard's path for four decades in one domain — and you've internalized it so deeply you don't even recognize it as mastery practice. You call it "staying sober." Leonard calls it "loving the plateau." It's the same thing.

The Honest Map
Business & building: Obsessive
New ventures & inner work: Dabbler risk
Emotional life & vulnerability: Hacker
Sobriety & showing up: Master

3 The Plateau Problem

Here's your central tension, and it's worth sitting with.

Leonard's entire book is about loving the plateau — the long stretches where nothing visible is happening, where practice continues without reward, where the only progress is invisible consolidation happening below the surface. His thesis: the people who endure and arrive at mastery are the ones who learn to find fulfillment in those flat stretches.

Now look at your wiring:

  • Thought 18 — decides by gut, instantly. Plateaus feel like stuck.
  • Physical Energy 97 — action-oriented, high-pace. Plateaus feel like death.
  • Insistence 97 — system-builder. Plateaus have no system to build.

Your entire neurological and psychological apparatus is designed to reject the plateau. When progress flattens, your body screams: do something. When the system has nothing to optimize, you build a new system. When a decision doesn't need to be made, you make one anyway because stillness feels like the forgotten kid being forgotten again.

The Bridge You Already Built
Forty years of sobriety is forty years of loving the plateau.

Think about it. Most of sobriety is not dramatic. It's not the first 90 days. It's not the big anniversary. It's Tuesday. It's the 14,600th day where nothing special happens and you just don't drink and you show up to the meeting and you call your sponsor and you do the inventory. That's the plateau. You've been on it for four decades.

You never called it mastery practice. You called it staying sober. But Leonard would recognize you instantly: a man who chose the path, stayed on the path through years of apparent non-progress, found meaning in the daily practice itself, and kept showing up when the culture around him was chasing quick fixes and dramatic transformations.

The problem isn't that you can't love the plateau. You already do — in the one domain where you had no choice but to learn it. The problem is you haven't transferred that framework to business, to emotional growth, to presence, to grief. In those domains, you're still the Obsessive trying to sprint past the flat stretches.

The Reframe
You don't need to learn to love the plateau.
You need to recognize you've been doing it for 40 years
and apply the same discipline everywhere else.

One day at a time. That's not just AA wisdom. That's Leonard's entire thesis in five words. You already own it. Now use it.

4 Homeostasis — Rich's Version

Leonard says all systems — biological, psychological, social — resist change. Not just bad change. All change. The thermostat doesn't know if you turned it up or down; it just fights to restore the old setting. He calls this homeostasis, and he says it's the hidden reason most resolutions fail and most transformations collapse.

You are in the middle of more simultaneous change than most people face in a lifetime:

  • Walking away from a 17-year franchise identity
  • Jessica's ALS progression — the ground under your most important relationship is shifting daily
  • Building a new business platform from scratch
  • Deep identity reconstruction with Jon (Session 2 is rewriting origin stories)
  • Psychedelic integration (ibogaine, 5-MeO) — literal neurological rewiring

Leonard's framework predicts — with certainty — that your system will try to snap back. Here's what that will look like for you specifically:

The Alarm Signals

Alarm 1: The Insistence Lockdown Insistence 97 → Stress
When homeostasis kicks in, your first move will be to build more structure. Tighter systems. More detailed processes. More control. This is your thermostat trying to restore the old setting — because in the old world, systems meant safety. Watch for: creating elaborate frameworks for the new business before it needs them. Over-engineering process. Building the cage. Your Birkman Need is 7 — you need the door open. But under stress, you'll slam it shut.

Alarm 2: The Speed Override Physical Energy 97 + Thought 18
Your system's second move is acceleration. More activity. Faster decisions. Fill every gap with motion. You said it: "I speed up instead of slow down. The Undercover Agent disguises overwhelm as productivity." This is homeostasis weaponizing your greatest strength against you. The activity feels like progress. It's actually your system trying to avoid the discomfort of being in transition.

Alarm 3: The Isolation Retreat Social Energy 92/38 → withdrawal
You'll frame it as needing alone time — and you legitimately do. But there's a difference between recharging and hiding. Under homeostatic stress, your Social Energy gap flips: you'll stop reaching out, stop asking for help (the thing you identified as your 20% hammer), and retreat into self-sufficiency. The forgotten kid's oldest defense: if I don't need anyone, no one can forget me.

Alarm 4: The Emotional Shutdown Emotional Energy 23/51 → YELLOW stress
As change intensifies, your stoic exterior will harden. The grief about Jessica, the uncertainty about the new business, the identity questions from coaching — all of it will get processed through the detachment filter. You'll look fine. You'll sound fine. Inside, the emotional backlog will build until it finds a crack. Leonard says homeostasis doesn't distinguish between change for the better and change for the worse. Your emotional system will try to restore the old equilibrium of "I'm fine" regardless of whether you actually are.

Alarm 5: The Resistive Dig-In YELLOW stress behavior
Your Birkman stress color is YELLOW: resistive and over-controlling. When all the alarms fire at once, you'll dig in. Not on the right things — on the comfortable things. You'll resist the changes that are actually working (the vulnerability, the asking for help, the sitting with uncertainty) and double down on the changes that feel safe (new systems, new projects, new external builds). The system is smart. It won't fight you directly. It'll redirect your energy toward things that look like growth but are actually homeostatic defense.

Leonard's Five Guidelines — Applied

  1. Expect it. You're changing everything at once. Your system WILL push back. When the first alarm fires, don't panic. Name it: "That's homeostasis. That's my system doing its job."
  2. Negotiate, don't bulldoze. Insistence 97 wants to power through. Don't. When you feel the resistance, slow down. Ask: what is my system trying to protect? Usually it's protecting the forgotten kid. Thank it. Then choose anyway.
  3. Use your support system. Jon. Kasey. The five friends. AA community. This is not optional. You identified "ask for help" as the 20% hammer. Homeostasis will try to make you forget you said that. Don't let it.
  4. Keep the daily practice. Meditation. Inventory. Meetings. Presence with Jessica. These are your anchors. When everything else is shifting, the practice stays. That's not rigidity — that's the stable base from which change becomes possible.
  5. Commit to the long game. This reconstruction isn't a project with a completion date. It's a mastery path. One day at a time. You know how to do this.

6 The Master's Practice — What It Looks Like for Rich

What to Practice

Leonard says practice is a noun — a path you're on, not a task you complete. For you, mastery practice lives in three domains:

  • Presence. This is the main event. With Jessica, with your kids, with yourself. Not presence as a concept you talk about in coaching — presence as the thing you actually do when nobody's watching. Unloading the dishwasher with attention. Sitting with Jessica without an agenda. Being in your body when your body says pressure.
  • Vulnerability. Saying "I need help" is practice. Not once — regularly. To Kasey. To Jon. To the five friends. To the team. Each time it will feel unnatural because Self-Consciousness 1 will scream that you're exposing yourself. That's the practice.
  • Stillness. Not meditation as a box to check. Stillness as a domain of mastery — getting better at doing nothing. For a Physical Energy 97, this is the advanced course. Five minutes of genuine nothing. No phone. No planning. No agenda. Just being the one who was always already okay.

How to Love the Plateau

You already know how. You've been doing it for 40 years. The transfer is simple:

  • Sobriety framework: One day at a time → applied to building, to grief, to presence, to the new business. Not "where will this be in 5 years" but "did I show up today?"
  • Daily inventory: You already do this for sobriety. Extend it. Not just "did I stay sober" but "did I practice presence? Did I ask for help? Did I notice the agent?"
  • Meetings as plateau practice: AA meetings are Leonard's practice-as-noun in pure form. You go. You listen. You share. Nothing dramatic happens most days. That's the plateau. You love it enough to keep going back. Notice that. Name it. Use it as evidence that you can do this everywhere.

When to Push the Edge — and When to Surrender

  • Push the edge when the thing you're avoiding is internal. Asking for help. Showing emotion. Sitting with uncertainty. Saying "I don't know." These are your growth edges — the ones your system is designed to avoid.
  • Surrender when the thing you're chasing is external. Another system. Another launch. Another decision that could wait. When Thought 18 wants to decide right now, that's often the moment to surrender. Sleep on the big ones. Your Need 62 is telling you something your Usual behavior ignores.
  • The rule: If it scares your ego, push. If it feeds your ego, pause.

Handling the Homeostatic Snapback

It will come. Leonard guarantees it. Here's your protocol:

  1. Name it. "This is homeostasis. My system is doing its job." Not a failure. Not a sign you're broken. Systems behavior.
  2. Check the alarms. Am I building more structure than needed? Am I speeding up? Am I isolating? Am I going stoic? Am I digging in on the comfortable stuff?
  3. Call someone. Not after you figure it out. Before. This is the 20% hammer. Use it when the system is trying hardest to make you forget it exists.
  4. Return to practice. Not heroic effort. Just the daily thing. Inventory. Presence. Stillness. One day at a time.

The Role of the Fool

This is your hardest assignment. Self-Consciousness 1 means you have zero natural tolerance for looking incompetent. Assertiveness 72 means you're used to being the one who knows. The combination makes beginner status feel like a threat to your existence — because for the forgotten kid, competence was the only currency that bought belonging.

But here's what you told Jon: "I'm the one who shows up. Not because I have to. Because that's who I am."

The fool shows up too. The fool shows up especially when he doesn't know. The fool says "I don't know how to grieve this" and "I don't know how to build this" and "I don't know who I am without the franchise" — and stays in the room anyway. That's not weakness. Leonard says it's the highest form of mastery courage.

You've done it once. First day sober, 40 years ago. You were the fool. You didn't know anything. You showed up anyway. That's the white belt. You've already worn it. You can wear it again.
