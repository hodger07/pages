1
Pre-Arrival
Get your head right before you touch the door
▸
Mental & Physical Preparation
  • Mental End Zone Preparation — get your head right before you touch the door
  • Mindset: I am a trusted advisor protecting this customer’s home
  • Dial in your personal image: pressed shirt and pants, well-groomed, shined black boots, black belt
  • Vehicle clean, shiny tires, organized, presentable
  • Review customer history before arrival: prior calls, memberships, previous recommendations, known concerns, and notes
  • Phone and iPad on vibrate
  • Leave all problems in the van
  • Plan on being recorded visually and audibly by cameras everywhere on the property
  • Observe before you approach: neighborhood, cars, house, landscaping, pride-of-ownership signals, and anything that helps you understand what the customer values
Positive Vehicle Placement
  • Park in the street whenever possible with the van visible from the front door
  • Never park in the driveway
  • Do not block the mailbox, driveway, or fire hydrant
  • Position the van like a professional presence, not an inconvenience
  • Exit the vehicle quickly and move with purpose — park to front door in 30 seconds or less whenever possible
🎯 Key TakeawayYou are not a fixer. You are a trusted advisor. The call starts before you touch the door.
2
Approaching the Home
Walk with confidence — first impressions are everything
▸
Walking from Van to Door
  • Walk with confidence, good posture, SMILE
  • Fast brisk pace from van to door
  • Do NOT walk through grass or flower beds
  • Minimum required tools only — a doctor doesn't carry all his tools
At the Door
  • Knock on the door frame
  • Step back, 45-degree angle to the door
  • Tool bag on the ground
  • SMILE
3
Introduction & Entry
Set authority and warmth at the door
▸
The Introduction Script
Technician"Hi, my name is _______, I am with Mister Sparky! I'm here to see Mr/Mrs/Ms. Doe."
Technician"Good to meet you, Mr. [Surname]. I'm going to be your electrician today."
Technician"Thanks for having me out today. Do you mind if I call you Bob?"
Technician"Bob, is my van parked okay? It's not going to be in the way of anything?"
Technician"Do you mind if I come in?"
Technician"Okay great, let me put on my floor savers to protect your nice floors."
Control the Entry
Technician"Before I start touching anything, I’m going to ask a few questions, inspect the issue, show you what I find, and then give you clear options. Fair enough?"
⚠ ImportantThis is the first control point. You are polite, but you are setting the agenda.
Account Review / Recognition
Technician — Existing Customer"Bob, before we get started, I did take a minute to review your account history so I’m not walking in cold. I saw [membership / prior visit / previous recommendation / note], and I appreciate you having us back out. My goal is to build on what we already know about your home and make this as smooth as possible for you today."
Technician — New Customer"Bob, I see you haven’t used Mister Sparky before — is this your first time having us out? Thank you so much for giving Mister Sparky and myself the opportunity to take care of your electrical system today, make sure everything is safe and sound, and hopefully earn the right to be your electrician for life."
Technician — Inspection Setup"John, let me quickly share with you what’s going to happen during your service call today. Before I get started, I’m going to perform a high-level safety evaluation of your electrical system. The purpose of this is twofold: first, to ensure the safety of your family and your home; and second, to make sure it is safe for me to work on so I don’t get injured. Now, if I find any issues, would you like me to share those with you or should I keep them to myself? Okay, great."
Use SmartlyRecognize the account accurately. If they have history, reference the real history. If they are new, thank them for the opportunity and set the bigger standard: safe and sound today, electrician for life long-term.

Problem Discovery & Pre-Inspection
Find the problem, impact, risk, and desired outcome
▸

🎯 Key TakeawayTheir words become your close. Problem → duration → impact → desired outcome.
  • What happened?
  • How long has it been happening?
  • What has it interrupted or made harder?
  • What does fixed look like?
5
Structured Relationship & Control
Four questions. Four anchors. No freewheeling.
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayRelationship building is not small talk. These questions create anchors you will use later.
  • Ask the four questions one at a time
  • Write down the exact words they use
  • Do not interrogate or wander
  • Every later objection ties back to these anchors
6
Diagnostic Mini-Anchor
Protect the diagnostic before the customer minimizes it
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayElectrical problems can be simple at the device or part of a larger circuit condition. The diagnostic keeps us from guessing.
  • Explain circuit-level troubleshooting before price resistance starts
  • Use “condition,” “unsafe,” and “not recommended to defer” only when tied to observed facts
  • Everything short of “fire” unless there is a specific observed fire hazard
7
Full Value Anchors
Maintenance math after credibility, before options
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayThe anchors frame every objection. Do not debate from scratch later — callback to what they already agreed to.

  • 4% planning benchmark
  • Electrical exposure anchor
  • “We don’t want to touch that number today” anchor
8
Decision Maker Alignment
Two touches: early agenda, then before options
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayDo not let “I need to talk to my spouse” appear for the first time at the close.

  • Before options: is that person available now?

9
Teeing Up the Circuit Restoration
Use findings + recurrence + diagnostic credit
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayLet them connect the dots: same condition, same circuit, same diagnostic if it happens again.
  • Reconnect to their emotional driver
  • Show observed facts

10
Presenting the Options
Best / Better / Essentials — never yes/no
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayEssentials is the minimum safe option. If they reject full scope, you still leave them safer than you found them.
  • Best = most comprehensive
  • Better = solid single-circuit solution
  • Essentials = minimum safe option
  • Ask which option, then stop talking
11
Objection Handling
Tie back to anchors. Always.
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayNo freelancing. Every objection response starts with: “Earlier you told me…” / “We agreed…” / “Based on what I found…”
  • Too much money → value, gap, financing, minimum safe option
  • Spouse → callback to decision-maker anchor
  • Three bids → separate cosmetic shopping from active electrical condition
  • Think about it → isolate the real objection
12
Closing Techniques
Reduction close, payment path, clear next step
▸
Open Full Script →
🎯 Key TakeawayClose from anchors, not pressure. The customer’s prior words do the heavy lifting.
  • Find the gap
  • Reduce over useful life
  • Offer card or financing
  • If they will not buy full scope, downshift to minimum safe option
