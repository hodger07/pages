Pre-Arrival

Mental End Zone Preparation

Make sure the vehicle is clean, with shiny tires, organized, presentable. You must look and act the part too. Leave all problems in the van. Make sure to put your phone and iPad on vibrate.
2
Approaching the Home
▼
Walk with confidence, good posture, SMILE — this builds esteem. Have a fast brisk pace from your van to the customer's door. Clean approach to the door. Do not walk through grass or flower beds.

Minimum required tools only — you don't want them to think you are just a "fixer." You are a professional — just like a doctor doesn't walk in with all his tools, neither do you.

At the Door

  • Knock on the door frame
  • Step back, stand at a 45-degree angle to the door
  • Tool bag on the ground
  • SMILE

4
Rapport Building (F.O.R.M.)
▼
Technician
"Bob, since this is the first time we have worked together — would it be okay if we sat down for a few minutes to review everything where you are most comfortable in the house before I get started?"
This should not be pre-scripted. It needs to flow from you naturally. Build relationships based on F.O.R.M. Rapport will always follow this format!
F Family
O Occupation
R Recreation
M Material Possession
Technician
"Before we get started, can I ask you a few questions just to help me better serve you as a client?"
Technician
"If you don't mind, tell me about yourself and your family?"
Technician
"What do you enjoy doing the most in your life?"
Technician
"What drives you and makes you tick?"
Technician
"What do you enjoy the most and least of home ownership?"

When to Move On

  • They are providing good information beyond "yes / no"
  • You can feel they are connecting with you
  • You can tell they want and are ready to move forward

Rapport Building Scorecard

A = Activated and connected — you connected with what drives them AND connected personally or professionally
B = Activated only — found their driver but didn't personally connect
C = Asked about F.O.R.M. but didn't activate or connect
D = No rapport built — you acted as a tool (hammer), not a human
5
Setting Critical Anchors & Buyer Qualification
▼

You want to set two critical anchors here:

  • They must agree to the 4% rule
  • They must agree to 20-30% of the final amount as deferred electrical maintenance
Technician
"Quick question Bob, what is your strategy for maintaining your home and keeping it in tip-top shape?"
Bob
"I don't know that we really have any strategy to speak of. We just sort of fix what breaks, when it breaks."
Technician
"No problem, most of my customers answer the same way, this is completely normal."
Technician
"The experts say you should plan on 4% of the home value for all-in maintenance cost per year, would you agree with this?"
You do NOT move on until they agree with the percentage — no dollars at this point. When they do agree you MUST lock it in.
Bob
"I think that is what I've always been told…"
Technician
"Great, so you agree the right number is 4%?"
Bob
"Yes."
Technician
"Yes?! Ok, Great!"
Technician
"So Bob, what is the current value of your home?"
Bob
"Around $500,000 I think."
Technician
"Okay, and how old is your home?"
Bob
"10 years old as of last week."
Pull out a calculator — NOT your phone. You should carry a proper calculator. You're a professional and professionals have professional tools.

The Math

$500,000 × 4% = $20,000/year
$20,000 × 10 years = $200,000 total deferred maintenance
Technician
"Let me do some quick math Bob… So if we do the math based on the experts it pencils out to this number."
Show the customer the figure on the calculator.
Technician
"Now I don't need to ask you. I am sure you hate this number."
Bob
"Uh, yea… That's crazy."
Technician
"I do too, so let's just… for good measure cut the number in half."
This is the 2nd important anchor you MUST lock them on verbally. The number will vary between 20-30% based on value of house (lower = more, higher = less).
$200,000 ÷ 2 = $100,000
$100,000 × 25% (electrical) = $25,000 electrical deferred maintenance
Technician
"Of the 4% Bob, the experts say that 20-30% depending on value of house is allocated to electrical. So, if I do the math here on the number we cut in half, it comes out to $25K."
Technician
"Bob, based on experts, do you agree this is the right number for electrical deferred maintenance on a home with this value and age?"
They must say yes, then you repeat it back to them and lock it. Only when they submit and agree do you then re-align.
Bob
"Yes sir. I do believe that pans out. I have never seen the math on my house, but that seems right."
Technician
"Okay great, and we can both agree that you do not want to touch that number today, right Bob?"
Bob
"Hell no I don't!"
Technician
"Yes, I agree Bob, my goal is to de-risk you from all this deferred maintenance and not touch that number."

Future Risk Education

Technician

Wait for them to acknowledge before speaking.
Technician
"Well, here we are. We found it. But here's what's important — all the devices we pulled out are wired the same way. So let me ask you — what do you think the chances are that this same issue might happen on another device in the same circuit?"
Pause — let them answer. Guide if needed while nodding head up and down.
Bob
"Yes, I imagine it is likely, they are all the same age."
Technician
"So when it fails again, you know how we operate — we troubleshoot at the circuit level. What do you think we'd have to do again?"
Let them answer. DO NOT answer for them.
Bob
"Diagnose it again."
Technician
"Right. And there's a fee for that — more than you'd like to pay again, I'm sure."

Technician

Technician
"Is that something you'd be interested in looking at today?"

Bob
"I would like to see how much that would cost. Might as well."
Technician

Presenting the Options
▼
Technician
"Hey Bob, thanks for taking a few minutes with me today. My goal is simple — remember when I said earlier that I wanted to earn the right to be your electrician for as long as you own your home. You've shared your approach with me: you've focused your resources on investments, your kids' activities, and enjoying your home, which is great. You've got a beautiful place — you just haven't really had to put much into the 'bones' of the home yet. That's perfectly normal, and that's where today's conversation comes in."

Framing Home Maintenance

Technician
"The industry standard is to allocate roughly 4% of your home's value annually toward overall maintenance. That doesn't mean you're spending that amount with me today — it's just a ballpark for all upkeep, whether that's roofing, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, foundation work, fencing, or painting. Some years it's one big thing; some years it's smaller things. Today we're focusing on the electrical side."

Setting the Tone

Technician
"Before we dive in, I want to make sure you're comfortable stopping me at any point if I get too technical or start rambling. I'm not here to 'sell' you — I'm here to give you clear, straightforward options so you can make the best decision for your home. Fair enough?"

The Three Options

Technician
"I've put together three investment options for you: Best, Better, and Essentials. Think of them as different levels of scope — from the most comprehensive solution to just the essentials. Let's start at the top and work our way down."

⭐ Option 1 — BEST (Most Comprehensive)

"This covers the most work in one visit, maximizing efficiency and future-proofing your home. For example, we'd restore two circuits today. That means more reach and reliability — I've already got outlets and switches pulled, and since we know all the outlets are wired similarly, doing both now saves you the cost and hassle of a future call."

"It's like shopping at Costco — you may get more than you immediately need, but it's more efficient and cost-effective in the long run because you'll use it, and you get it at a better price per unit."

Option 2 — BETTER (Single Circuit + Safety)

"This is the same quality of work, just a smaller scope. We'd restore one circuit today and roll your $600 diagnostic fee into the repair cost — effectively cutting that fee in half for you."

Includes: grounding, surge protection, updated smoke detectors, GFCI bubble covers, and tested GFCIs in the bathrooms.

Option 3 — ESSENTIALS (Baseline Safety Only)

"This is the minimum I'd recommend — taking care of those critical safety measures I just mentioned, without any circuit restoration work. It addresses immediate safety needs but leaves the functional upgrades for another time."

The Close

Technician
"So that's the overview: Best for maximum efficiency and long-term coverage, Better for solid safety plus one circuit restoration, and Essentials for safety only. Which option feels right for you today?"
When you ask "which option feels right for you today" — you must not speak until the answer. Expect a long pause. This is normal. Let them process and think. They MUST talk first.
11
Objection Handling
▼

The Four Personality Types

Driver

Direct and straight-forward objections

Influencer

Sarcastic objections

Stable

Avoids conflict — uses smokescreens

Compliant

Asks detailed questions

45%+ of the population falls into a High Stability personality. The "I need to talk to my spouse" objection often comes from this type — it's frequently a smokescreen, not a real condition. You must master handling this objection.

"I Need to Talk to My Spouse"

When to address it: Before it occurs! If you observe the client is a High S personality and constantly bringing up their spouse, that means:

  • She has not reached a comfort level with you — work on the relationship!
  • You are probably going to hear the spouse objection

Rule: When repairs exceed single-spouse approval, you need the involvement of the other spouse.

Discover If It's a Smokescreen

Bob
"I need to talk to my spouse."
Technician
"That makes a lot of sense Bob. Do you think she will have some questions about what we covered today?"
Bob
"Yes I do."
Technician
"What type of questions do you think she will ask?"
Bob
"She'll probably ask about the price."
Technician
"Well, that's an easy one to answer. Do you think she will ask about the technical side of the installation process?"
Bob
"I don't think so. Probably not, she is more concerned about price."
Technician
"Oh. Well what do you think she will say about that price?"
Bob
"It's too high."
Technician
"I bet that is exactly what she'll say. Bob, what do you say about the price?"
Bob
"Well, uhm. This is a little awkward. Uh, I think the price is a little high."
You have just discovered that the spouse is probably not the issue. It's the client's perception of your value and price. Continue to address the price objection.

If It's a Real Condition (Not a Smokescreen)

You have 2 choices:

  • Option A: Get the spouse involved over the phone — they keep the diagnostic credit
  • Option B: Set an appointment to come back and visit together
You can only roll the diagnostic into the circuit restoration if you do it now. If you must come back, you lose the efficiency, and they lose the savings. Doing it now is a win-win.
Technician
"Absolutely — I'd want to do the same thing. This is an investment in your home, and it's worth making together. Just so we don't lose momentum and so your spouse has the same info you do, would it be possible to give them a quick call or put them on speaker? Sometimes it helps if I'm here to answer any questions in real time instead of you having to re-explain later."

"I Want to Think About It"

Technician
"That's fine, Bob, I understand. This is an important choice for you. You wouldn't take your time thinking it over unless you were seriously interested, would you?"
Bob
"That's right. I'm interested; I just need some time to think it over."

Clarify the Truth

Technician
"Just so that I have it clear in my mind, you do feel that your home needs the options I have shown you, don't you?"
Bob
"Yes I do. You have proven that very clearly."
Technician
"Oh good, because I was worried that I offended you, and you were trying to kindly get rid of me. That's not the case, is it?"
Bob
"No, No, No. I like you!"
Technician
"Oh good, because the last thing I would want to do is offend you!"

Check Off the List

Technician
"Since it is my job to answer all of your concerns, will you tell me what it is you were wanting to think over?"

Then systematically eliminate:

  • "Was it the options I recommended?"
  • "Is the quality of my work a concern?"
  • "What about the reputation of our company?"
  • "What about the guarantees and warranties?"
  • Reconfirm other value points…
Technician
"Ok Bob. Level with me — is it the investment that is causing your concerns?"
Bob
"That's a big part of it of course. It's just a lot of money."

Available Closes

The Options Close — show an item of lesser price
The Service Agreement Close — give a discount with Just Fix It App Download and Fix More Save More
The Reduction Close — break it down to the ridiculous

The Reduction Close (Example)

Technician
"So, really, we are talking about $800, aren't we?"
Technician
"All right. I think we should look closely at the total over the life of your electrical system. Here."
Hand Bob the calculator.
Technician
"You said you plan on living in the home for at least the next 7 years, right?"
Technician
"Our Circuit Restoration has a minimum of 7 years of worry-free service with no repairs, that's guaranteed here in writing."
$800 ÷ 7 years = $114/year
$114 ÷ 365 days = $0.31/day
Technician
"The peace of mind alone will pay for that minor daily difference. Let me ask you one last thing."
Technician
"Won't this Circuit Restoration, with: the peace of mind, low maintenance, zero repairs for 7 years, our great company to stand behind the installation, and all of our in-writing assurance guarantees be worth more than $0.31 a day of your time?"
Bob
"Yes, I think it will."
Technician
"Great! Then we are on the same page, aren't we?"

The Easy Choice Close

Technician
"By the way, did you want to put this on a credit card you get points on or did you prefer to use our money?"
This is the Easy Choice Close. It is a whole lot easier to decide on a simple thing like "Card or my money" than deciding on the grand total of the install.
