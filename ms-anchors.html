Phase 5

Setting Critical Anchors

Buyer Qualification — Establishing the 4% & Electrical Standards
🎯 Phase Goal Two anchors. Both must be locked verbally before you move on. The math does the selling — you just hold the calculator.
The Two Critical Anchors Lock both before proceeding

You must secure both verbal agreements before moving forward. These anchors frame all future conversations around price.

1
Agree to 4%

Customer agrees that 4% of home value per year is the industry standard for total home maintenance.

2
Agree to 20–30% Electrical

Customer agrees that 20–30% of that 4% is allocated to electrical — based on house value and age.

Anchor 1: The 4% Standard Script + dialogue
Opening the Anchor Conversation ▸
Technician

"Quick question Bob, what is your strategy for maintaining your home and keeping it in tip-top shape?"

Customer — Bob "We just sort of fix what breaks, when it breaks."
Technician

"No problem, most of my customers answer the same way — this is completely normal."

Technician

"The experts say you should plan on 4% of the home value for all-in maintenance cost per year. Would you agree with this?"

⚠ ImportantDo NOT move on until they agree with the percentage. No dollar amounts at this point — percentages only. Dollars come after they've agreed to the concept.
Locking the Agreement ▸
Customer — Bob "I think that is what I've always been told…"
Technician

"Great, so you agree the right number is 4%?"

Bob "Yes."
Technician

"Yes?! Ok, Great!"

Running the Numbers — First Calculation Use a physical calculator
Getting the Home Info ▸
Technician

"So Bob, what is the current value of your home?"

Bob"$500,000"
Technician

"How old is your home?"

Bob"10 years"
📝 NotePull out a calculator — NOT your phone. A physical calculator signals professionalism and keeps this from feeling casual. This moment matters.
The Math: Deferred Maintenance ▸
📊 Deferred Maintenance Calculation
Home Value$500,000
Annual Maintenance (4%)$500,000 × 4% = $20,000/yr
Home Age10 years
Total Deferred Maintenance$200,000
Technician

"Let me do some quick math Bob… it pencils out to this number."

📝 NoteShow the figure on the calculator. Hand it toward them. Let the number land. Don't rush past it.
Technician

"Now I don't need to ask you. I am sure you hate this number."

Bob"That's crazy."
Technician

"I do too, so let's just cut the number in half."

Anchor 2: The Electrical Percentage Lock the electrical allocation verbally
📝 NoteThis is the second anchor. You must lock it verbally. 20–30% of the 4% is allocated to electrical, depending on house value and age. Do not move on until they agree.
Electrical Math & Script ▸
📊 Electrical Deferred Maintenance
Total Deferred (halved)$200,000 ÷ 2 = $100,000
Electrical Allocation (25%)$100,000 × 25% = $25,000
Electrical Deferred Total$25,000
Technician

"Of the 4%, Bob, the experts say 20–30% depending on house value is allocated to electrical. The math comes to $25,000."

Technician

"Bob, based on the experts, do you agree this is the right number?"

⚠ ImportantThey must say yes. Repeat it back. Lock it verbally. Don't let this slide with a nod or a shrug — get the word "yes" out of their mouth before continuing.
Bob"Yes sir."
Technician

"And we can both agree you don't want to touch that number today, right?"

Bob"Hell no!"
Technician

"Yes, I agree Bob — my goal is to de-risk you from all this deferred maintenance and not touch that number."

🔑 Key TakeawayYou've now established that $25,000 is the "scary number" — the full deferred electrical exposure. Everything you quote from here forward will feel small by comparison. That's the anchor working.
