Phase 5
Setting Critical Anchors
Buyer Qualification — Establishing the 4% & Electrical Standards
The Two Critical Anchors

You must secure both verbal agreements before moving forward. These anchors frame all future conversations around price.

1
Agree to 4%

Customer agrees that 4% of home value per year is the industry standard for total home maintenance.

2
Agree to 20–30% Electrical

Customer agrees that 20–30% of that 4% is allocated to electrical — based on house value and age.

Anchor 1: The 4% Standard
Technician

"Quick question Bob, what is your strategy for maintaining your home and keeping it in tip-top shape?"

Customer — Bob
"We just sort of fix what breaks, when it breaks."
Technician

"No problem, most of my customers answer the same way — this is completely normal."

Technician

"The experts say you should plan on 4% of the home value for all-in maintenance cost per year. Would you agree with this?"

⚠ Important
You do NOT move on until they agree with the percentage. No dollar amounts at this point — percentages only. Dollars come after they've agreed to the concept.
Customer — Bob
"I think that is what I've always been told…"
Technician

"Great, so you agree the right number is 4%?"

Bob
"Yes."
Technician

"Yes?! Ok, Great!"

Running the Numbers — First Calculation
Technician

"So Bob, what is the current value of your home?"

Bob
"$500,000"
Technician

"How old is your home?"

Bob
"10 years"
📝 Note
Pull out a calculator — NOT your phone. A physical calculator signals professionalism and keeps this from feeling casual. This moment matters.
📊 The Math — Deferred Maintenance
Home Value $500,000
Annual Maintenance (4%) $500,000 × 4% = $20,000/yr
Home Age 10 years
Total Deferred Maintenance $200,000
Technician

"Let me do some quick math Bob… it pencils out to this number."

📝 Note
Show the figure on the calculator. Hand it toward them. Let the number land. Don't rush past it.
Technician

"Now I don't need to ask you. I am sure you hate this number."

Bob
"That's crazy."
Technician

"I do too, so let's just cut the number in half."

Anchor 2: The Electrical Percentage
📝 Note
This is the second anchor. You must lock it verbally. 20–30% of the 4% is allocated to electrical, depending on house value and age. Do not move on until they agree.
📊 The Math — Electrical Deferred Maintenance
Total Deferred (halved) $200,000 ÷ 2 = $100,000
Electrical Allocation (25%) $100,000 × 25% = $25,000
Electrical Deferred Total $25,000
Technician

"Of the 4%, Bob, the experts say 20–30% depending on house value is allocated to electrical. The math comes to $25,000."

Technician

"Bob, based on the experts, do you agree this is the right number?"

⚠ Important
They must say yes. Repeat it back. Lock it verbally. Don't let this slide with a nod or a shrug — get the word "yes" out of their mouth before continuing.
Bob
"Yes sir."
Technician

"And we can both agree you don't want to touch that number today, right?"

Bob
"Hell no!"
Technician

"Yes, I agree Bob — my goal is to de-risk you from all this deferred maintenance and not touch that number."

🔑 Key Takeaway
You've now established that $25,000 is the "scary number" — the full deferred electrical exposure. Everything you quote from here forward will feel small by comparison. That's the anchor working.
