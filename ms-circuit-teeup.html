Phase 9

Teeing Up the Circuit Restoration

The customer should talk themselves into the restoration. You ask the questions — they arrive at the conclusion. NEVER answer your own question.
Technician"Bob, you got a minute?"
Pause — wait for them to say yes.
Technician"Okay, great. Can we step into the room where the issue was? I'll show you what I found."

The Finding

Technician"Great news. I found the problem with the circuit. It has to do with the devices — they're all quick wired. You don't need to worry about the technical side of that — basically, it's an easy wiring method that works fast upfront, but it's not very durable long-term."
Technician"You are following me so far?"
Technician"Either way — I got you fixed up today. You'll be back to normal, everything's working. So, I'm sure that's a relief."
Pause — SMILE and wait for them to respond. Reconnect to their emotional driver.
Bob"YES! That is great! So, I can start baking my cookies again! Fantastic! Thank you!"

Future Risk Education

Technician"Remember at the beginning when I said the only way I could confidently tell you I found the problem, fixed the problem, and help make sure it never happens again — was by doing this diagnostic?"
Wait for them to acknowledge before speaking.
Technician"Well, here we are. We found it. But here's what's important — all the devices we pulled out are wired the same way. So let me ask you — what do you think the chances are that this same issue might happen on another device in the same circuit?"
Pause — let them answer. Guide if needed while nodding head up and down.
Bob"Yes, I imagine it is likely, they are all the same age."
Technician"So when it fails again, you know how we operate — we troubleshoot at the circuit level. What do you think we'd have to do again?"
Let them answer. DO NOT answer for them. Silence is your most powerful tool here.
Bob"Diagnose it again."
Technician"Right. And there's a fee for that — more than you'd like to pay again, I'm sure."

The Soft Pitch

Technician"What if I told you we could credit back your diagnostic fee toward a full solution — to make sure this doesn't happen again — while everything's already open and exposed?"
Technician"Is that something you'd be interested in looking at today?"
Don't rush this moment. Don't speak until they answer you. The silence after this question is doing the selling.
Bob"I would like to see how much that would cost. Might as well."
Technician"Great. Give me just a few minutes — I'll pull everything together and come sit down with you to go over the options."
They just asked YOU to sell them. That's the whole play — the customer arrived at the conclusion themselves. You never "pitched" anything. You asked questions and they connected the dots.
