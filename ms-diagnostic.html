Phase 6

Position the Diagnostic

Protect diagnostic value before the customer minimizes it
🎯 Phase Goal You are setting the expert frame: electrical conditions can hide upstream or downstream. The diagnostic keeps you from guessing and protects the customer from a patch that fails later.
The Position the Diagnostic Say this before price resistance starts
Technician

"Bob, before I start guessing at the outlet, I need to understand whether this is a simple device issue or part of a larger circuit condition. Electrical problems can hide upstream or downstream. The diagnostic is how I make sure we’re not just patching the symptom and leaving the real condition behind."

⚠ Field RealityCustomers may minimize the issue or challenge the diagnostic cost. This anchor explains why the diagnostic exists before they start controlling the conversation.
Professional Disclosure Rule Do not ask permission to share facts
Technician

"My job is to show you what I find, explain what it means, and give you clear options. You decide what to do, but I don’t want you uninformed about an electrical condition in your home."

Language GuardrailAllowed when tied to observed facts: unsafe condition, safety concern, required to make this safe/reliable, not something I recommend deferring, minimum safe option. Avoid "fire" unless there is a specific observed fire hazard you can document.
Find the Right Setting Sit down — slow everything down
Seating Script
Technician

"Bob, is there a good place we can sit for a couple of minutes to go over what I found?"

📝 NotePause while walking. Let them lead you somewhere comfortable. Sitting down signals this is a real conversation, not a quick handoff. It slows everything down in the best way.
Reporting the Panel Findings Lead with good news
Panel Report Script
Technician

"Good news — I checked your breaker panel, and everything looks solid. A couple things worth noting, but nothing urgent. For now, I want to focus on the issue you called us for."

📝 NotePause here. Let them respond. This moment of "good news" builds confidence and goodwill. Let it breathe.
Radical Transparency — Root Cause Honesty builds more trust than false certainty
Transparency Script
Technician

"Now — I want to be fully upfront with you — I wasn't able to isolate the exact root cause just yet… and that's okay. That's pretty normal in systems like this."

📝 NoteReassure with body language. Nod confidently. Keep a calm, steady tone. You're not uncertain — you're being honest about the nature of electrical systems. There's a difference.

Circuit-Level Education Bring Bob into your world simply
System Explanation Script
Technician

"Are you familiar at all with how your electrical system is laid out?"

Bob"Not really."
Technician

"Your home's electrical system is one big system — but it's made up of smaller systems called circuits. That's where I dive in and troubleshoot — at the circuit level."

The Two Reasons Script
Technician — The Two Reasons

"I do it this way for two reasons. First — so I can look you in the eye and tell you I've actually found and fixed the issue. And second — so I can help ensure it doesn't happen again."

1

So I can look you in the eye and tell you I've actually found and fixed the issue — not guessed at it.

2

So I can help ensure it doesn't happen again — because one fix isn't enough if the system's at risk.

⚠ Important — Eye ContactEye contact is essential when delivering the two reasons. You're making a personal commitment to this person. Look them in the eye. This isn't a script — this is a promise.
Technician

"Does that make sense so far?"

📝 NoteThis check-in keeps them engaged. It's not rhetorical — genuinely pause and let them confirm before moving forward.
Gaining Buy-In — The Signature Moment Stop talking. Let them sign.
Technician
"If we want to move forward, all I need from you is a signature right here and I can get started right away for you."
— The Moment of Commitment —
📝 NotePause. Hand them the iPad or device. Do not rush this moment. Do not fill the silence with more words. The customer has everything they need to decide. Your job right now is to stop talking.
🔑 Key TakeawayThe diagnostic presentation is about building trust through honesty — not about having all the answers. Admitting you need to dig deeper at the circuit level is exactly what makes you credible.
