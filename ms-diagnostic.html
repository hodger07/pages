Phase 8
Presenting the Diagnostic
Educating the customer — building trust through transparency
Find the Right Setting
Technician

"Bob, is there a good place we can sit for a couple of minutes to go over what I found?"

📝 Note
Pause while walking. Let them lead you somewhere comfortable. If they offer a seat — take it. Sitting down signals that this is a real conversation, not a quick handoff. It slows everything down in the best way.
Reporting the Panel Findings
Technician

"Good news — I checked your breaker panel, and everything looks solid. A couple things worth noting, but nothing urgent. For now, I want to focus on the issue you called us for."

📝 Note
Pause here. Let them respond. Don't rush to the next thing. This moment of "good news" builds confidence and goodwill. Let it breathe.
Addressing the Root Cause — Radical Transparency
Technician

"Now — I want to be fully upfront with you — I wasn't able to isolate the exact root cause just yet… and that's okay. That's pretty normal in systems like this."

📝 Note
Reassure with body language. Nod confidently. Keep a calm, steady tone. You're not uncertain — you're being honest about the nature of electrical systems. There's a difference. Confidence in the process, not false certainty about the outcome.
Bob
"What do we do?"
Circuit-Level Education

This is where you bring Bob into your world in a way that makes sense to him. Don't talk over him. Make it simple, make it real.

Technician

"Are you familiar at all with how your electrical system is laid out?"

Bob
"Not really."
Technician

"Your home's electrical system is one big system — but it's made up of smaller systems called circuits. That's where I dive in and troubleshoot — at the circuit level."

Technician — The Two Reasons

"I do it this way for two reasons. First — so I can look you in the eye and tell you I've actually found and fixed the issue. And second — so I can help ensure it doesn't happen again."

1

So I can look you in the eye and tell you I've actually found and fixed the issue — not guessed at it.

2

So I can help ensure it doesn't happen again — because one fix isn't enough if the system's at risk.

⚠ Important — Eye Contact
Eye contact is essential when delivering the two reasons. You're making a personal commitment to this person. Look them in the eye. This isn't a script — this is a promise.
Technician

"Does that make sense so far?"

📝 Note
This check-in keeps them engaged. It's not rhetorical — genuinely pause and let them confirm before moving forward.
Gaining Buy-In — The Signature Moment
Technician

"If we want to move forward, all I need from you is a signature right here and I can get started right away for you."

— The Moment of Commitment —
📝 Note
Pause. Hand them the iPad or device. Do not rush this moment. Do not fill the silence with more words. The customer has everything they need to decide. Your job right now is to stop talking.
🔑 Key Takeaway
The diagnostic presentation is about building trust through honesty — not about having all the answers. Admitting you need to dig deeper at the circuit level is exactly what makes you credible. Customers trust honest professionals over confident guessers.
