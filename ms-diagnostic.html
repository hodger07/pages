Phase 6

Position the Diagnostic

Protect diagnostic value before the customer minimizes it
🎯 Phase Goal You are setting the expert frame: electrical conditions can hide upstream or downstream. The diagnostic keeps you from guessing and protects the customer from a patch that fails later.
1. Return From the Inspection Lead with calm confidence
Technician

"Great news, everything looks really good. I did find a few small items we can discuss in a minute, but right now I want to get into why you called us out here today. Do you have a minute to sit down and review the next steps?"

Field NoteDo not dump panel findings or small issues first. Lower the customer's guard, then re-center on the reason they called.
2. Re-Center on the Original Issue Safety evaluation vs. diagnostic
Technician

"The safety evaluation did not uncover anything obvious that would explain the issue you're having. That's actually very common, because most electrical problems are not visible from the panel. They're usually hidden behind walls, inside boxes, at devices, or somewhere along the circuit."

ImportantThe customer needs to understand this: the safety evaluation and diagnostic are not the same thing. The safety evaluation checks visible safety concerns. The diagnostic finds the hidden problem they called about.
3. Position the Single-Circuit Diagnostic Name the next step clearly
Technician

"Before we talk about next steps — can I ask: are you familiar at all with how your electrical system is laid out?" (Pause for a quick yes/no.)

Technician

"Okay — your home's electrical system is one big system — but it's made up of smaller systems called circuits. That's where I dive in and troubleshoot — at the circuit level."

Technician

"I do it this way for two reasons. First — so I can look you in the eye and tell you I've actually found and fixed the issue. And second — so I can help ensure it doesn't happen again." (Eye contact here is essential — connect personally.)

4. What the Diagnostic Includes Make the work feel real
Technician

"What we'll do is isolate the circuit and start working through the devices connected to it. That can include removing fixtures, switches, outlets, or hardwired connections so we can inspect the wiring, open connections, splices, and devices."

Technician

"We're looking for things like failed devices, loose connections, damaged splices, burnt wiring, or anything else that would cause the problem you're experiencing."

5. Why This Is the Right Process No guessing with electrical
Technician

"This is the only accurate way to verify what's happening on this circuit. It lets us basically x-ray the circuit, inspect it, validate that it's in safe working order, and make sure we're not leaving behind the same problem for you to deal with again later."

Technician

"Anything less thorough is really just guessing, and I don't want to guess with your electrical system."

Language GuardrailDo not overuse fear. If needed, say: “If we do less than this, there's a chance we leave an unresolved issue behind.” Avoid making the customer feel pressured or scared.
6. Present the Investment Flat fee, then stop
Technician

"The investment for the single-circuit diagnostic is a flat fee of $XXX.00. That does not go up or down based on how long it takes us to find the problem."

Technician

"That fee guarantees that we will identify the problem on this circuit."

7. Clarify What Is and Is Not Included Prevent surprises
Technician

"If it's something minor, like reconnecting wires or replacing wire nuts, that's included with the diagnostic."

Technician

"If we find a failed device, damaged wiring, a bad fixture, or something that requires a larger repair, I'll stop, show you exactly what we found, and give you options before doing any additional work."

8. Risk Reversal Take the fear out
Technician

"And if we're wrong — if the repair we recommend does not fix the issue — then that repair is on us. Totally free."

9. Questions + Authorization Ask, pause, authorize
Technician

"What questions do you have about that?"

PauseAnswer clearly. Do not rush. Do not talk past the question.
Technician
"With your authorization, I'll go ahead and get started on the single-circuit diagnostic."
— The Moment of Commitment —
🔑 Key TakeawayPositioning the diagnostic is about trust through clarity. The customer is buying certainty, not electrical trivia.
