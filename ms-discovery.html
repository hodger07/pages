Phase 4

Problem Discussion & Pre-Inspection

Understanding the issue — and the person behind it
🎯 Phase Goal Find the customer's exact words: what happened, how long, what it has interrupted, and what fixed looks like. These are the anchors you will use later.
Agenda Control Before Inspection Set the rails early
Technician

"Bob, before I start touching anything, I’m going to ask a few questions, inspect the issue, show you what I find, and then give you clear options. Fair enough?"

Technician

"And if we find something meaningful today, who else needs to be part of that conversation?"

⚠ Control PointThis is not a spouse trap. It is an early anchor. If they later say, "I need to talk to my spouse," you tie back to what they told you here.
Customer Walks You to the Issue Your approach as they show you the problem
1
Follow Their Lead
Let them show you. Don't rush ahead to the panel or the outlet. Walk with them. This moment builds trust.
2
Ask, Don't Assume
You don't know what happened yet. Ask — every time. Even if it looks obvious, the context matters.
3
Listen for Emotion
The frustration in their voice, the worry in their eyes — these are signals. Store them. You'll use them to activate later.
Discovery Questions 4 anchors to capture
🕐
Technician
"Before I touch anything, tell me exactly what has been going on."
⚡
Technician
"How long has this been happening?"
🔄
Technician
"What has this interrupted or made harder for you?"
🏠
Technician
"If we get this handled the right way today, what does fixed look like to you?"
Compassion & Credibility Three essential statements
Compassion Statement
Technician — Compassion

"Thanks for walking me through that. I know this can be frustrating, especially when it interrupts your day or creates worry."

Credibility Statement
Technician — Credibility

"I've been doing electrical work for [X] years now and I've seen issues like this plenty of times. You're in good hands."

Chief Goal Statement
Technician — Chief Goal

"My goal today is to earn the right to be your electrician for as long as you own the home — everything I do today will be centered around that chief goal."

📝 NoteSay it like you mean it. It reframes your entire visit — you're not here to fix one thing and leave. You're here to begin a relationship.
Tie Back to Their Words ⚡ CRITICAL
Callback Script
Technician

"Bob, earlier you told me this has been interrupting [their exact words]. I’m going to keep that in mind while I inspect this, because the goal is not just to get power back — it’s to make sure we are not leaving the real condition behind."

Listen for what it costs them emotionally — not just functionally:

Example Response A

"Can't make toast for my youngest kid on weekends."

Example Response B

"Can't bake — and that's my hobby. It's been months."

⚠ Important — Must ActivateCircle back to what they just told you. Their words are your anchors. Do not create a new argument later when you can tie back to what they already said.
Final Credibility & Transition to Inspection Hand off and head to work
Company Credibility
Technician — Credibility Statement

"I've been with Mister Sparky for [X] years. The reason I've stayed is because we put pride into doing things right — safely, professionally, and with integrity."

Transition to Inspection
Technician — Inspection Setup

"John, let me quickly share with you what’s going to happen during your service call today. Before I get started, I’m going to perform a high-level safety evaluation of your electrical system. The purpose of this is twofold: first, to ensure the safety of your family and your home; and second, to make sure it is safe for me to work on so I don’t get injured. Now, if I find any issues, would you like me to share those with you or should I keep them to myself? Okay, great."

Bob"I'll continue working in my office."
Technician

"Perfect — I'll circle back once I'm finished."

🔑 Key TakeawayAlways give them the choice. Never assume they want to follow you around. Respecting their time and space builds trust faster than any pitch.
