Phase 7
Problem Discovery & Pre-Inspection
Understanding the issue — and the person behind it
Customer Walks You to the Issue

As the customer shows you the problem area, you're not just looking at the electrical issue — you're listening, observing, and building the full picture of what this means to them.

1
Follow Their Lead
Let them show you. Don't rush ahead to the panel or the outlet. Walk with them. This moment builds trust.
2
Ask, Don't Assume
You don't know what happened yet. Ask — every time. Even if it looks obvious, the context matters.
3
Listen for Emotion
The frustration in their voice, the worry in their eyes — these are signals. Store them. You'll use them to activate later.
Discovery Questions
🕐
Technician

"When did you first start noticing this?"

⚡
Technician

"Did anything unusual happen — breaker trip, burning smell, flickering?"

🔄
Technician

"Has this happened before, or is this the first time?"

🏠
Technician

"Any other areas in the house doing something similar?"

Show Compassion & Build Credibility
Technician — Compassion

"Thanks for walking me through that. I know this can be frustrating, especially when it interrupts your day or creates worry."

Technician — Credibility

"I've been doing electrical work for [X] years now and I've seen issues like this plenty of times. You're in good hands."

Technician — Chief Goal

"My goal today is to earn the right to be your electrician for as long as you own the home — everything I do today will be centered around that chief goal."

📝 Note
The "chief goal" statement is critical. Say it like you mean it. It reframes your entire visit — you're not here to fix one thing and leave. You're here to begin a relationship.
The Activation Question — CRITICAL
Technician

"What do you do when you can't use the outlet in the kitchen?"

Listen for what it costs them emotionally — not just functionally:

Example Response A

"Can't make toast for my youngest kid on weekends."

Example Response B

"Can't bake — and that's my hobby. It's been months."

⚠ Important — Must Activate
Circle back to what they just told you. Connect the repair directly to their emotional driver and personal goals. If Bob said he can't bake, the fix isn't just fixing an outlet — it's getting Bob back to his hobby. You must activate before moving on.
Final Credibility & Transition to Inspection
Technician — Credibility Statement

"I've been with Mister Sparky for [X] years. The reason I've stayed is because we put pride into doing things right — safely, professionally, and with integrity."

Technician — Transition

"Bob, thanks for taking the time. I'm going to start with a quick inspection — you're welcome to come along or hang out here. Whatever works best for you."

Bob
"I'll continue working in my office."
Technician

"Perfect — I'll circle back once I'm finished."

🔑 Key Takeaway
Always give them the choice. Never assume they want to follow you around. Respecting their time and space builds trust faster than any pitch. Give them control where it doesn't cost you anything.
