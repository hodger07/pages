Phases 11 & 12

Objection Handling & Closing

Tie back to anchors. Always.
Anchor Callback System No freelancing
⚡ Governing RuleEvery objection response ties back to what the customer already agreed to: the decision maker, the problem history, the 4% math, the diagnostic cost basis, the larger risk number, and the minimum safety standard. Do not argue from scratch.
  • Decision-maker anchor: "Earlier you told me you were the decision maker on this."
  • Problem anchor: "Earlier you told me this has been happening for years."
  • Diagnostic anchor: "Earlier we talked about how everyone has the same cost basis. In Houston and Dallas, it usually costs $500–$700 just to get anything meaningful done at the home, regardless of the trade."
  • 4% math anchor: "We used home value × 4%, cut that number in half to be conservative, counted 20% of that conservative number for electrical, then multiplied it by the age of the home. On a $1,000,000 home, that is $40,000 → $20,000 → $4,000/year electrical → 20 years = $80,000 deferred electrical maintenance exposure. That is not a scare number — that is the conservative planning number."
  • Zero-index anchor: "Most people accidentally index electrical maintenance to $0 because they do not see it every day. That does not mean the risk is $0."
  • Safety anchor: "This is the airbags, seat belts, good tires, headlights, and brake lights conversation. Paint and cosmetics can wait. Safety cannot."
Trainer NoteThe customer is not choosing between buying and not buying. They are choosing whether to keep ignoring an electrical system that has already shown symptoms. The tech's job is to make the safety decision clear without panicking them.
Safety Math Foundation 4% → half → 20% electrical
Home value × 4% annual maintenance benchmark
Cut that number in half = conservative maintenance exposure
Take 20% of the conservative number = annual electrical planning number
Multiply by age of home = deferred electrical maintenance exposure
Example: $1,000,000 × 4% = $40,000 → half = $20,000 → 20% electrical = $4,000/year → 20 years old = $80,000
Technician

"Bob, this is why I walked through the math earlier. We take the home value times 4%, cut that result in half to be conservative, take 20% of that conservative number for electrical, then multiply it by the age of the home. On a $1,000,000 home, that is $4,000 per year of conservative electrical exposure. If the home is 20 years old, that is $80,000 of deferred electrical maintenance exposure. Most people don't budget for that because the system is hidden in the walls, so they accidentally treat the value and cost like zero. It isn't zero."

Technician

"With a car, nobody says brakes, tires, airbags, seat belts, headlights, and brake lights are optional just because the paint looks fine. A home is the same way. The bare minimum electrical safety items are surge protection, grounding, GFCIs, working smoke detectors, and proper circuit-level diagnostics when circuit problems show up — not just changing devices and hoping."

Safety BoundaryThe worst-case reason this matters is fire and life safety. Do not throw around fear language casually. Tie it to observed facts: missing protection, failed devices, open grounds, non-working smoke detectors, circuit symptoms, heat, arcing, burning smell, tripping, or repeated failures.
The Four Personality Types Same facts, different delivery

Driver

Need: control + speed.
Use: direct facts, clear choice, no rambling.
Line: "You told me you decide. Here are the safety facts and the minimum I recommend."

Influencer

Need: trust + confidence.
Use: reassurance, social proof, simple story.
Line: "Most homeowners don't think about electrical until it shows symptoms. Yours has."

Stable

Need: safety + no pressure.
Use: calm tone, family protection, spouse clarity.
Line: "I'm not trying to pressure you. I am trying to make sure your family is safe."

Compliant

Need: logic + proof.
Use: inspection report, math, observed defects.
Line: "Here is the observed condition, here is the risk, and here is the code/safety reason."

Trainer RuleDo not change the recommendation by personality type. Change the delivery. Same safety floor, same math, same decision-maker standard.
"I Need to Talk to My Spouse" Decision-maker anchor
Why This HappensThis objection is either real decision-maker absence or a softer way to say price/value concern. We ask for decision makers early so they cannot use this at the end without clarifying what changed.
Bob"I need to talk to my spouse."
Technician — Core Response

"That makes sense. Earlier you told me you were the decision maker if we found something meaningful. So let me ask it clearly: is your spouse actually the decision maker here, or is the real concern the investment?"

If Real Decision Maker

"No problem. Since this is a safety decision, let's get them the same information while I'm here. Can we put them on speaker for two minutes so I can show them the inspection report and answer questions directly?"

If Price Concern

"I appreciate you saying that. Then let's talk about the investment directly. Earlier we used home value × 4%, cut that number in half, counted 20% of that for electrical, then multiplied it by the age of the home. This option is how we handle the documented safety items now instead of letting the home drift toward that bigger deferred number."

Personality Adjustments
  • Driver: "Totally fair. Quick yes/no — are they the decision maker or is this about the investment?"
  • Influencer: "I get it. Most couples want to feel good together on home decisions. Let's bring them in so nobody has to re-explain this later."
  • Stable: "I respect that. I also do not want you carrying a safety decision alone. Let's include them calmly while the facts are fresh."
  • Compliant: "Good. They should see the same report, photos, and safety reasoning. Let's walk them through the data."
"I Need to Get Three Bids" Cosmetic shopping vs. safety condition
Bob"I need to get three bids."
Technician — Core Response

"I understand comparing bids. If this were paint, flooring, countertops, or something cosmetic, I would tell you to compare. Electrical safety is different. Based on what I found today, this is more like tires, brakes, airbags, seat belts, headlights, and brake lights. We need to make the home electrically safe first."

Technician

"Also, everyone has the same basic cost basis. In Houston and Dallas, it usually costs $500–$700 just to get anything meaningful done at the home, regardless of trade. The difference is not whether there is a cost — the difference is whether the home is left safe today."

Technician — Minimum Close

"If you still want bids on the larger scope, I understand. But I do not recommend leaving without at least the minimum safety items handled: surge protection, grounding, GFCIs, working smoke detectors, and proper circuit-level diagnostics where we found circuit symptoms."

Personality Adjustments
  • Driver: "Compare the big stuff if you want. My recommendation is we handle the minimum safety items today."
  • Influencer: "I want you to feel good about who you choose. I also don't want you sleeping on a known safety condition."
  • Stable: "I am not asking you to rush the whole project. I am asking you not to defer the safety floor."
  • Compliant: "Get bids on scope. But the observed safety items are documented here, and deferring them does not make the risk disappear."
"It's Too Much Money" Re-index value from $0
Bob"It's too much money."
Technician — Core Response

"I hear you. Most homeowners accidentally index electrical maintenance to $0 because nothing looks broken until something stops working. But earlier we did the conservative math: home value × 4%, cut that number in half, then 20% of that conservative number for electrical, multiplied by the age of the home. That still leaves a real deferred electrical safety number."

Technician

"Earlier you said you hated that big number, and we agreed we wanted to de-risk you from that number. This option does that. It is not about spending money for fun — it is about keeping the electrical system from becoming a larger, more expensive, and potentially dangerous problem."

Technician — Downshift Without Losing Safety

"If Best is more than you want to do today, we can talk about Better or Essentials. But I do not recommend doing nothing. Essentials is the minimum safe option. This is one of those things the home has to afford at some level because safety cannot stay at zero."

Personality Adjustments
  • Driver: "I understand. Then choose the level. Best, Better, or minimum safe — but zero is not my recommendation."
  • Influencer: "Nobody loves spending money on electrical. The win is peace of mind and knowing the home is safe."
  • Stable: "I know it feels heavy. My job is to help you pick the safest responsible step, not force the biggest one."
  • Compliant: "Let's go back to the math and the inspection report. Which safety item do you disagree with?"
"I Want to Think About It" Time has already passed
Bob"I want to think it over."
Technician — Core Response

"I understand. This is an important choice. But earlier you told me this has been happening for years. In a way, we have already been thinking about it for years by not spending money to keep the home safe."

Technician

"Unfortunately, based on what I found today, the time has come and gone on the safety part. We have to put airbags, seat belts, good tires, headlights, and brake lights on this car regardless of paint or cosmetic condition. Your home needs the electrical version of that for you and your family."

Technician — Isolate

"So help me understand what you need to think about: is it the safety need, the investment, or which option is the right level?"

Personality Adjustments
  • Driver: "What exactly are we thinking over — need, price, or option?"
  • Influencer: "I want you comfortable. The part I do not want is for this to become another thing that gets pushed off for another year."
  • Stable: "No pressure. But I do need to tell you clearly: my safety recommendation is not to defer this."
  • Compliant: "Let's identify the missing information. If I can answer it now, would you be comfortable choosing the minimum safe option today?"
Customer Minimizes the Issue Do not let them index it to $0
Bob"It’s probably not that big of a deal."
Technician

"I understand why it feels that way. Electrical is hidden, so it is easy to treat the value and the risk like zero. But the condition I found today is not zero. The reason we do circuit-level diagnostics is because changing devices without understanding the circuit can leave the real problem behind."

Technician

"I am not here to scare you. I am here to tell you the truth: electrical problems can become fire and life-safety problems when they are ignored. Based on what I observed, I recommend we at least make the home electrically safe today."

Language RuleUse fire/life-safety language only when tied to observed facts. Do not create fear. Create clarity.
Closing Techniques Safety-first closes for 2026
2026 RuleOld pressure closes feel dated, especially on grudge purchases in a tight economy. Do not make the customer feel trapped, clevered, or talked into something. A good close for an average technician is calm, specific, and tied to a safe next step: safety floor, line-item agreement, decision-maker confirmation, payment path, or minimum safe option.
Close Menu — Use These Instead
Safety Floor Close — separate must-do safety items from optional comfort or upgrade items
Line-Item Buy-In Close — confirm agreement on each observed defect before discussing total investment
Minimum Safe Option Close — if Best or Better is too much, protect the home at the lowest responsible level
Decision-Maker Confirmation Close — bring the real decision maker into the facts before the tech leaves
Payment Path Close — after agreement, make card, ACH/check, or financing the next simple choice
Safety Floor Close — Full Script
Technician

"Bob, I am going to separate this into two buckets so it is easier to decide. These items are the safety floor based on what I found today. These other items are improvements that can be scheduled if you want to phase the work."

Technician

"My recommendation is not that you have to do everything today. My recommendation is that we do not leave the home below the minimum safe level."

Technician — Ask

"Are you comfortable handling the safety floor today and then deciding later on the remaining items?"

Line-Item Buy-In Close — Full Script
Technician

"Before we talk about the total, I want to make sure the recommendation makes sense. You saw the missing/failed item here, correct? And you understand why I am recommending it be corrected?"

Bob"Yes."
Technician

"Good. So the question is not whether this item matters. The question is whether we do the full option or the minimum safe version today."

Trainer NoteThis keeps weak technicians out of debate mode. Confirm the facts one item at a time. No clever language, no pressure.
Minimum Safe Option Close — Full Script
Technician

"If the larger option is more than you want to take on today, I understand. The responsible downshift is not doing nothing. The responsible downshift is the minimum safe option."

Technician

"That means we handle the items that protect the home and people first: the documented safety items, the required protection, and the diagnostics needed to avoid guessing."

Technician — Ask

"Do you want to move forward with the minimum safe option today?"

Decision-Maker Confirmation Close — Full Script
Technician

"Earlier you told me you could make the decision if we found something meaningful. If someone else needs to be part of the decision, let's include them now while the inspection report and photos are in front of us."

Technician

"I do not want you trying to re-explain electrical safety from memory tonight. Two minutes on speaker is cleaner and fairer to everyone."

Technician — Ask

"Should we call them now, or are you comfortable approving the safety floor yourself?"

Payment Path Close — Full Script
Technician

"If you are comfortable with the safety floor, the next question is just how you want to handle it. Some homeowners use a card, some use ACH or check, and some use financing to keep cash free."

Technician — Ask

"Which payment path is best for you today?"

ImportantOnly use this after the customer has agreed to the scope. Do not use payment choice to imply agreement before agreement exists.
🎯 Key TakeawayThe close is not a trick. Confirm scope, protect the safety floor, then make the next step simple.
