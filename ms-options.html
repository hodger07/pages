Phase 10

Presenting the Options

Three options. Start from the top. The Costco analogy makes "Best" feel logical, not expensive. The silence after "which option" does the closing.
Technician"Hey Bob, thanks for taking a few minutes with me today. My goal is simple — remember when I said earlier that I wanted to earn the right to be your electrician for as long as you own your home. You've shared your approach with me: you've focused your resources on investments, your kids' activities, and enjoying your home, which is great. You've got a beautiful place — you just haven't really had to put much into the 'bones' of the home yet. That's perfectly normal, and that's where today's conversation comes in."

Framing Home Maintenance

Technician"The industry standard is to allocate roughly 4% of your home's value annually toward overall maintenance. That doesn't mean you're spending that amount with me today — it's just a ballpark for all upkeep, whether that's roofing, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, foundation work, fencing, or painting. Some years it's one big thing; some years it's smaller things. Today we're focusing on the electrical side."

Setting the Tone

Technician"Before we dive in, I want to make sure you're comfortable stopping me at any point if I get too technical or start rambling. I'm not here to 'sell' you — I'm here to give you clear, straightforward options so you can make the best decision for your home. Fair enough?"

The Three Options

Technician"I've put together three investment options for you: Best, Better, and Essentials. Think of them as different levels of scope — from the most comprehensive solution to just the essentials. Let's start at the top and work our way down."

⭐ Option 1 — BEST (Most Comprehensive)

Technician"This covers the most work in one visit, maximizing efficiency and future-proofing your home. For example, we'd restore two circuits today. That means more reach and reliability — I've already got outlets and switches pulled, and since we know all the outlets are wired similarly, doing both now saves you the cost and hassle of a future call."
Technician"It's like shopping at Costco — you may get more than you immediately need, but it's more efficient and cost-effective in the long run because you'll use it, and you get it at a better price per unit."

Option 2 — BETTER (Single Circuit + Safety)

Technician"This is the same quality of work, just a smaller scope. We'd restore one circuit today and roll your $600 diagnostic fee into the repair cost — effectively cutting that fee in half for you."

Includes: grounding, surge protection, updated smoke detectors, GFCI bubble covers, and tested GFCIs in the bathrooms.

Option 3 — ESSENTIALS (Baseline Safety Only)

Technician"This is the minimum I'd recommend — taking care of those critical safety measures I just mentioned, without any circuit restoration work. It addresses immediate safety needs but leaves the functional upgrades for another time."

The Close

Technician"So that's the overview: Best for maximum efficiency and long-term coverage, Better for solid safety plus one circuit restoration, and Essentials for safety only. Which option feels right for you today?"
When you ask "which option feels right for you today" — you must not speak until they answer. Expect a long pause. This is normal. Let them process and think. They MUST talk first. The silence is doing the work.
