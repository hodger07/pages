Phase 10

Presenting Inspection Report & Options

Inspection report first. Then Best / Better / Essentials.
🎯 Phase Goal Present the inspection report clearly, then offer Best / Better / Essentials. No extra framing. No second setup.
Setting the Stage Reconnect to rapport before presenting
Opening Script
Technician

"Bob, before I show you the options, I want to tie this back to what you told me earlier. You told me [customer anchor], and during the inspection I found [observed condition]. I’m going to walk you through what I found first, then I’ll give you clear options so you can choose what makes the most sense for your home today."

Tone-Setting Script
Technician

"Before we dive in, I want to make sure you're comfortable stopping me at any point if I get too technical or start rambling. I'm not here to 'sell' you — I'm here to give you clear, straightforward options so you can make the best decision to make your home electrically safe. Fair enough?"

Introducing the Three Options Frame before you present
Technician

"I've put together three investment options for you: Best, Better, and Essentials. Think of them as different levels of safety benefit — from the most complete way to make the home electrically safe to the minimum items I would recommend today. Let's start at the top and work our way down."

How to PresentDo not charge through the list. Present one line item at a time. After each safety benefit, stop and get verbal buy-in before moving to the next line: “Does that make sense?” / “Would you want that protected?” / “Is that something you’d want handled while we’re here?” If they do not say yes, slow down and clarify before continuing.
⭐ Option 1 — BEST Most comprehensive — the Costco approach
⭐ Best — Most Comprehensive
Best Option Script
Line-Item RuleThe tech must earn yes on each line item. Read the first safety benefit, pause, get agreement, then move to the next. This is not a presentation to survive — it is a series of small yeses.
Technician

"This covers the most work in one visit, maximizing efficiency and future-proofing your home. For example, we'd restore two circuits today. That means more reach and reliability — I've already got outlets and switches pulled, and since we know all the outlets are wired similarly, doing both now saves you the cost and hassle of a future call."

Technician — Costco Analogy

"It's like shopping at Costco — you may get more than you immediately need, but it's more efficient and cost-effective in the long run because you'll use it, and you get it at a better price per unit."

Best Option CloseIf they agreed to every line item, do not keep presenting. Close on Best: “Based on what you said yes to on each item, Best is the option that gets all of those safety benefits handled today. Do you want us to move forward with that?”
If They HesitateAsk: “Which safety benefit would you not want us to take care of today?” Then listen. Their answer tells you whether to keep Best, downshift to Better, or isolate the real objection.
Option 2 — BETTER Single circuit + safety — diagnostic fee rolled in
Better — Single Circuit
Better Option Script
Technician

"This is the same quality of work, just a smaller scope. We'd restore one circuit today and roll your $600 diagnostic fee into the repair cost — effectively cutting that fee in half for you."

Includes: grounding, surge protection, updated smoke detectors, GFCI bubble covers, and tested GFCIs in the bathrooms.

Option 3 — ESSENTIALS Baseline safety only — no restoration
Essentials — Safety Only
Essentials Option Script
Technician

"This is the minimum safe option I’d recommend today. It takes care of the required safety items without the full circuit restoration. It does not solve everything, but it leaves the home safer than I found it."

The Close ⚡ Ask. Then stop talking.
Close Script
Technician

"So that’s the overview: Best for maximum efficiency and long-term coverage, Better for a solid single-circuit solution, and Essentials as the minimum safe option. Based on what you told me and what I found, which option feels right for you today?"

🤐 STOP TALKING. Wait until they speak first. The silence is doing the selling.
⚠ ImportantWhen you ask "which option feels right for you today" — you must not speak until they answer. Expect a long pause. This is normal. Let them process and think. They MUST talk first.
🔑 Key TakeawayThe Costco analogy reframes "Best" from expensive to efficient. Present all three in descending order and let the contrast do the work.
