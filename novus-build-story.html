How Dad Built His Operating System

Step by step — the coaching framework, the AI prompts, and what came out of it

1. The Starting Point: Why Jon, Why Now

Your dad hired Jon Roberts at Novus Global for an 18-session Meta Performance™ coaching contract. Not because something was broken. Because he wanted to finally build the thing underneath everything else — the operating system that runs the leader, the father, the man.

Jon's framing from session one set the tone for everything that followed:

"I am led by Self, supported by all my parts."
The goal isn't to fix what's broken — it's to remember who you already are.

Jon also had a rule about AI from the start. Your dad was already deep into building with AI tools, and Jon saw the opportunity — and the risk.

"It's a backboard for data and illumination. Rich owns the work — AI reflects, it doesn't replace."

And then Jon asked the question that started everything:

"What does the mature version of you look like? Not what he does — who he IS."

That question — who he is, not what he does — became the thread that ran through every session, every homework assignment, every late-night AI conversation that followed.

2. System A vs. System B — The Core Discovery

This is the foundation. Everything else builds on this.

Step 1: Jon asked your dad to describe what drives him when he's at his best versus when he's running on stress. Two completely different states came out — same guy, same work ethic, but powered by totally different fuel.

Step 2: Jon named them. System A = "have-to" fuel. Little Rich driving — produce to earn your place, control everything because if you don't hold it together it falls apart like childhood did. System B = "get-to" fuel. The mature Rich, building from wholeness instead of wound.

Step 3: The key insight that landed:

"The ACTIONS can look identical from the outside. Only I know which fuel is in the tank."

This is the part that changes everything. Your dad could run the same meeting, make the same decisions, close the same deals — and in one version he's building from abundance, and in the other he's running from a wound. The outside world can't tell the difference. Only he can.

Step 4: Jon gave him the nuclear practice: At any moment — morning, noon, end of day — ask: which system am I running? Not to judge it. Just to know.

Where AI came in

Between sessions, your dad used AI as a "backboard." He'd describe a recent meeting or conversation in detail and ask the AI to reflect back which system was running based on the evidence. Not therapy. Pattern recognition. The AI could spot System A fingerprints — control language, urgency that wasn't warranted, subtle defensiveness — that are hard to see from inside the moment.

3. The System B Descriptors — Building the Character

This is where it became real work.

Step 1: Jon's homework after Session 2: "Describe System B using adjectives — build the character of the mature Rich."

Step 2: First attempt with AI as thinking partner: generic words came out. "Intentional." "Present." "Focused." Fine words. Not the thing.

Step 3: The breakthrough — realizing the descriptors needed to be in order, like the 12 Steps. Skip #1 and everything below it is System A in disguise. The sequence matters because each one requires the ones before it to be true.

Step 4: The AI prompt that cracked it open:

"If System B is a person, what are the first 7 words you'd use to describe him — and in what order would they need to be true for the next one to be possible?"

That reframe — not just what are the words, but what order do they need to be true — produced the final seven:

  1. 1 ENOUGH — I have value before I produce. Nothing I build or lose changes this. (This is the only one that must be worked perfectly — it's the foundation.)
  2. 2 HERE — I exist before you notice. I don't need the room to see me to know I'm in it.
  3. 3 CURIOUS — I ask before I fix. When pressure hits, I open up instead of clench.
  4. 4 CLEAR — I say it once, clean, no shrapnel. The truth delivered without the damage.
  5. 5 GRATEFUL — I know what things cost. That's not a practice — it's what's left when the noise stops.
  6. 6 BUILDS — I build toward something, not away from something. From wholeness, not from wound.
  7. 7 CHOOSES — Every day, I choose this. One day at a time. System B is a decision, not a destination.

The 12-Step DNA is all over this. "Enough" is Step 1 — surrender. "Chooses" is the daily reprieve. Your dad has 40 years of sobriety, and the architecture of recovery shaped this entire framework without anyone planning it that way.

4. The IFS Crew Map — Session 5 Breakthrough

Jon introduced Internal Family Systems (IFS) and merged it with the System A/B framework. This is where it went from concept to cast of characters.

The idea: you're not one person. You're a crew. The question is who's driving.

Here's the crew your dad named in session:

THE KING — Self. The true leader. System B is what it looks like when he drives. Not a role he performs — who he actually is when the noise clears.
LITTLE RICH — The exile. The wound. Not invisible to the world — invisible to the specific humans who were supposed to see him first. This is the part that System A protects at all costs.
FRED / DUDE — The Manager. Builds systems, controls outcomes proactively, keeps Little Rich from getting hurt. Your dad's Birkman Insistence score is 97 — this is that fingerprint. Fred builds the fortress before anyone can attack it.
DICK — The Firefighter (push mode). Steamrolls. Wins the room, loses the relationship. No dimmer switch. New role given: Conqueror on demand — new frontiers only, not daily ops.
TOM — The Firefighter (withdraw mode). Full disappear, no warning, maximum every time. New role given: Intel, not decisions.
"I am led by Self, supported by all my parts."

The goal is for The King to mature and LEAD the crew — not destroy them. Every part exists for a reason. They kept your dad safe when he needed it. Now The King leads, and they serve in new roles.

Jon's homework after that session: Talk to Tom and Fred. Get to know the crew. You're leading them now.

5. "No One Has It Better Than Me"

Jon's total-presence phrase. Your dad pushed back at first — sounds like competition, like a comparison game.

Jon clarified: it's Frankl-level. Not "I beat everyone." It's something deeper:

"I am fully connected to my choice in this moment, regardless of hardship."

Viktor Frankl — the psychiatrist who survived the concentration camps — wrote that they can take everything from you except one thing: your choice of how to respond. That's what this phrase means. Not that life is easy. That you're fully in it.

The challenge Jon left your dad with: Structure life so your friends say, "Wait — you've got all this and you STILL say no one has it better?" And your dad answers why from a real place. Not a highlight reel. A real place.

6. How AI Fit Into This Work

Not a replacement for Jon. A thinking partner between sessions.

Here's what your dad actually did:

1. After each coaching session, he'd download the transcript to AI and ask: "What did I learn? What's still unresolved? What should I bring to Jon next time?"

2. He'd run System A/B self-audits — describe a recent meeting in detail, and the AI would reflect back which system was running based on the evidence.

3. He'd use AI to stress-test the System B descriptors: "Challenge these. Tell me where they're System A in disguise."

4. He used the AI to build the visual documents — the System B operating system visual, the crew map, the dashboards that capture the whole framework.

The prompts that produced breakthroughs

"If you were Jon Roberts, what would you challenge me on based on everything I just told you?"
"Describe the difference between how Dick and Tom would handle this situation — and which one I'm actually being right now."
"Read these 7 descriptors and tell me: is 'Enough' really first? What breaks if it isn't?"
"What would Little Rich say about the fact that I haven't told this story to anyone?"

That last one hit different. The AI reflected back that Little Rich — the part that felt unseen — would probably say: it's about time someone told the story. Which is part of why this page exists.

And the line that captures all of it:

"The goal isn't to kill System A. The goal is for The King to take the throne. The boy is growing up. He's finally taking the throne."
