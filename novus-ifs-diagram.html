Dad's Operating System — How It Works and Where It Came From

In Session 5 with Jon Roberts, your dad learned something that changed the whole framework: you're not one person. You're a crew. The question isn't whether the parts exist — it's who's driving.

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is a model from psychology: every person has parts — a manager, protectors, an exile (the wound), and Self (the true leader). Your dad was first introduced to IFS during integration counseling before he traveled to Canada for intensive medicine work. Jon re-introduced it in Session 5 and connected it to the System A/B framework they'd been building together. That's when your dad named the parts — live, in session — and the two frameworks became one.

Jon Roberts, Session 5: "I view myself way more as a team, rather than just as an individual. I am led by Self, supported by all my parts."

What follows are two diagrams. The first is how the system runs by default — what Jon calls "System A." The second is what the Novus work is building — "System B." Same crew. Different leader.

Diagram 1: How Most People Operate

This is the default. For most high-performers — your dad included — the Manager runs the show, the Firefighters take over under pressure, and the real wound stays buried. The King is asleep. Nobody asked him to lead.

Rich, Session 5: "Let's just name him Harry. So Harry is in charge of systems."

The Moment It Clicked

This whole crew map came from one session — Session 5 with Jon Roberts. Your dad named Harry and Tom right there in the conversation. He'd been living with these parts his entire life but had never given them names, never separated them from himself, never asked: who's actually driving right now?

That's the shift. Not getting rid of the parts. Not fighting them. Naming them, knowing them, and letting the King lead.

Jon Roberts: "System B is the captain. Your soul, your true choice is coming in. He's okay, he's enough."
