Session 2 — February 20, 2026

Rebuilding the Engine — Why slowing down felt like breaking down

What This Session Was About

This was the session where the real work started to hurt. Your dad came in conflicted — he'd spent the days since Session 1 doing the deep homework Jon assigned, and it cracked him open. He was sitting in a more reflective state than he'd ever allowed himself, and the unfamiliar stillness was creating physical tension. He could feel it in his breath.

The heart of it: your dad's entire life has run on what Jon calls System A — the "do-have-be" engine. Produce results → earn your place → then maybe, someday, you get to just be. Lil' Rich (the 8-year-old version of your dad that figured out how the world works) built that engine, and it's been running flawlessly for decades. Nobody outworks it. Nobody outproduces it. But it redlines the car every single lap.

Jon introduced System B — a completely different engine. Not "do-have-be" but "be → do intentionally → have what matters." The actions can look identical from the outside. The difference is what's fueling them. System B runs on clarity, presence, and choice instead of pressure, scarcity, and "I must produce to be valuable."

The hard part — and your dad was brutally honest about this — is that System B feels foreign. Like writing with your non-dominant hand. His productivity, which has always been his hallmark, had slowed down in the two weeks since starting this work. And when Lil' Rich stops producing, Lil' Rich doesn't feel valuable. That's the old wiring talking. Jon named it plainly: "Productivity is one of Lil' Rich's hallmarks. Best in the world at it."

Key Breakthroughs

  • System A isn't optional anymore. Your dad said something profound: after the loss of his son, after what he experienced in Canada with ibogaine, System A stopped being a viable long-term option. "Once you know the lie, you never unsee it." He compared it to an alcoholic who's been to an AA meeting trying to go back out and drink — the awareness ruins the illusion forever.
  • The discomfort IS the mile marker. The tightness in his chest, the feeling of being unproductive, the tears that came during homework — Jon reframed all of it. "The raw vulnerable is the mile marker on the path." The fact that it hurts means it's working.
  • System B is a choice, not a sentence. Jon pushed back when your dad framed it like he had to go to System B. "I believe you're a powerful human. You can go back to the old one. You can do the new one. You can sit on your ass and do nothing." The point: System B isn't chosen out of desperation. It's chosen because it's the greatest calling he's ever had.
  • Practice, not perfection. Jon used the racing metaphor: System B is a new car. You don't know how to drive it yet. Take clunky laps. Bring a mechanic. You're allowed to be inefficient. "Lil' Rich, we're not alone this time."

What Jon Said That Landed

"Every day he wakes up and he answers a question: Who am I? Remember who you are — that's not looking to the past. That is a 'Hey, Rich, remember that the one thing in life that you are actually in control over every day is who you choose to be.'"
"Lil' Rich — he's not bad at all. The system's not bad. You walk over there, you grab his hand and go, 'Hey buddy, you're not alone anymore. Everything you needed — I'm here. And I'm not sending you away. You're not bad, you're not wrong, you're not broken. It's you and me. Now — who do we get to be?'"
"System A is going to say, 'I need to do 100 things this week to be good.' System B is going to say, 'Hey Jon, there's really only three things that are going to move the needle.' But Jon, it feels so foreign to only be committed to three things — that feels criminal."

Homework Jon Gave

  • Describe System B using adjectives — build the character of the mature Rich. Not realistic, not who you are today — the dream. The perfect lap. "Build me the perfect Rich."
  • Review Session 2 notes and respond where it feels right
  • Identify 2-3 action items for the next seven days — big or small — and take action in those areas from System B. Jon's definition of productivity: Did you do this?

Jon also recommended the book Mastery by George Leonard — a short, no-frills book about the art of practice and staying on the plateau. The word Jon put on his wall that week: Practice.
