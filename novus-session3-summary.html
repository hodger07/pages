Session 3 — March 3, 2026

The Pain of System A, the Practice of B, and "What Is"

What This Session Was About

Your dad came into this session emotional. Jon asked him about his current relationship with System A — the old operating system, the one built on pressure, production, and proving your worth — and your dad's answer was immediate and honest: there's pain there. Not intellectual understanding of pain. Real, present, in-the-body pain.

Jon noticed something important: your dad kept checking with himself whether it was okay to feel what he was feeling. "Is it okay for me to be in pain about this? Yeah, I think it's okay." Jon called it out gently — "You're using it as punctuation. We don't need the punctuation. We got it." The feelings are allowed. No permission needed.

The session moved through three big areas. First, honoring where System A lives right now — it's pain and resentment, and that's the honest truth. The long-term vision is gratitude and connection to it, but that's not today. Second, the nuclear practice: the single most important thing your dad can do every day is notice which system is running him in any given moment. Not 100 things. One thing. Third, Jon introduced the concept of "what is" — first-principle thinking applied to relationships, commitments, and self. Strip away the stories, the projections, the protective fantasies. What's actually here?

There was also a moment where your dad talked about already practicing System B in real meetings — bringing curiosity instead of steamrolling, asking questions instead of dictating. He'd already shared the agreements-vs-expectations framework with his leadership team. The work was moving from theory to practice.

Key Breakthroughs

  • The plateau concept clicked. When System A kicks in, it rips you off the plateau you're building on. And there's a real cost to getting back on. Your dad connected it clearly: "Once that old version of you kicks in, I don't think you can coexist on the other plateau." The cost isn't just the moment — it's the streak you break and the energy it takes to rebuild it.
  • The "unproductive" feeling is a sign it's working. Jon got excited when your dad used the word "unproductive" about his recent weeks. That down-feeling in the reinvention phase is expected. You're a beginner at System B. Beginners feel clunky. That's the plateau from Mastery — you live on the plateau, and the feeling of "nothing's happening" is actually where the growth occurs.
  • Agreements vs. Expectations. Your dad took what Jon taught him about agreements and brought it to his leadership team. Instead of expectations (which breed resentment and excuses), he introduced agreements — you either kept it or you didn't, no stories, no morality attached. "It takes the right or wrong, good or bad person out of it. People just self-segment where they land."
  • "What Is" as a way of being. Jon's phrase for first-principle living. Most humans live in 80% projection — stories, fantasies, protective narratives. "What is" strips that away. The Golden Gate Bridge is a bridge. The water in the cup is water. A commitment kept is kept; a commitment broken is broken. Your dad connected it immediately: "It's first-principle thinking."

What Jon Said That Landed

"System B plateau — that's up here. I can't wait till we get there. Because frankly, that's where we can use your ability to grind. It'll be like, 'Oh Jon, I'm grinding, but it's the most life-giving stuff in the world.'"
"The most nuclear action Rich does every day is, in the present moment, becoming aware of which system is running him. That's it. That's the one thing. If you become a master at that — everything else follows."
"I don't believe all action is created equal. I sit here and watch people grind at not-force-multiplying actions. If we could pinpoint the one thing — the one thing that if you become a master at..."

Homework Jon Gave

  • Keep exercising the daily System A/B awareness check — morning, before meetings, after hard moments, end of day. Which system is running?
  • Finish the System B descriptors draft — have a working version of the "character sheet" for the mature Rich by next session
  • Practice "what is" in real conversations — strip away story, get to the commitment. Did I keep it or didn't I?
  • Keep sharing openly about the journey with the people around him — not oversharing, but authentic transparency about "I'm in reinvention"
