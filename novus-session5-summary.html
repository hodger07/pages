Session 5 — March 19, 2026

The King and His Crew — impact, triggers, and "no one has it better than me"

What This Session Was About

This session had a different energy. It had been a while since the last one — Session 4 got canceled when your dad had to step in at work during spring break. Jon opened by being honest about his own brain: "You were two minutes late today. Guess where my mind was going. I hope he's okay." He used his own story-making as a teaching moment — even coaches generate stories when information is missing.

Your dad came in doing the work. He'd been practicing System B in real meetings, using AI agents as thinking partners (not to do the work for him — as a "backboard" for illumination), and building out the System B descriptors Jon had assigned. He was enjoying it. Something was shifting. But the session went deeper than practice.

Jon introduced the concept of impact as the organizing word. Not "what do I accomplish" but "what impact do I have on my grandkids, my kids, my close friends, the 50 people who work for me?" And he pushed into something your dad had been dancing around: the triggers. What specifically kicks System A back into gear? Your dad named two clearly — failure (or the anticipation of failure) and not feeling approved, valued, respected. Not "no's" — those don't bother him. But a "no" that carries "because you're a piece of shit" underneath? That's a different animal.

Then came the big metaphor that Jon had been sitting with for a month: the King and his crew. Drawing from IFS therapy (Internal Family Systems — a framework your dad had first encountered during integration counseling before his medicine work in Canada, and which Jon now re-introduced and connected to the System A/B work), Jon described the inner cast of characters as a pirate ship. System A parts are the crew — warriors, lawyers, strategists — who've been protecting the boy prince his whole life. System B is what happens when the prince grows up, takes the throne, and starts leading the crew instead of being protected by them. "The boy is finally growing up. He's finally taking the throne."

Key Breakthroughs

  • Your dad named his triggers precisely. Failure — real or anticipated — and not feeling valued/respected/approved. Jon shared his own: when people tell him no and he feels he deserves a yes, and when he doesn't feel appreciated. They're different triggers, same mechanism. System A roars to life to protect the wound underneath.
  • The Birkman tension made real sense. Your dad connected his personality profile (Insistence score of 97 — highest need for building systems) to the crew metaphor. "Fred" builds systems — but rejects external systems imposed on him. Your dad called it plainly: "That's a fucking hot mess. That's a fucking problem. I need to sort that out." But then he immediately reframed it in Jon's language: "Let's just say that's part of my team."
  • System B starts with "I'm enough" — or it's a disguise. Your dad laid out his working model of System B in three layers: (1) I'm okay the way I am right now, full stop. (2) Who I actually am — curiosity, presence, depth. (3) Action — what I do. But they must happen in that order. "If I'm curious and I build things but I don't accept who I am — it's immediately System A. It's a disguise."
  • "No one has it better than me" — not competition, presence. Your dad pushed back on this phrase at first — it sounded like scarcity, like comparison. Jon clarified: it's Viktor Frankl. It means "I am fully connected to my choice in this moment, regardless of hardship." Not that life is easy. That you're fully in it. Your dad got there: "That's the missing part."
  • Open but not unlocked. Your dad drew a beautiful line about vulnerability going forward. He doesn't want to be a locked vault. He also isn't going to be wide open to everyone. "I want my grandkids to know me. I want to be approachable, a soft soul. But there's a sacredness to the deep places." Jon honored it: "Not locked — private. Deep. Holy. The inner sanctum."

What Jon Said That Landed

"The boy is finally growing up. He's finally taking the throne, he's finally maturing, he's finally stepping out. And it's kind of discombobulating for the crew, because they're like, 'Wait, we're not going to run all our same plays.' And the boy-prince-king is like, 'No guys, we're a team. We're still going to go on epic adventures. But you don't have to go alone anymore.'"
"What impact will I have on others, on my grandkids, on my kids, on my close friends? ... Historically, I always win. And there's a System A that gets it done and creates an impact. The question is — is that the impact you want?"
"How do you wake up every day and say, 'No one in the world has it better than me'? Regardless of challenge, regardless of hardship. And let me tell you why."

Homework Jon Gave

  • Try the 5-minute alarm system — set an iPhone alarm 5 minutes before every meeting. Remove the mental load of remembering. "When that alarm goes off, I have all the time in the world."
  • Keep exercising System A/B daily — which system is running? Morning, midday, after meetings, end of day.
  • Chat with Tom and Fred and the gang. Get to know the crew. You're leading them now. Not destroying them — leading them.
  • Keep using the AI tools for the deep work — journals, personality profiles, musing. Not to replace the work, but as a force multiplier for self-discovery.
