Archived Option B Detail

The current financial structure was built for a smaller, simpler operation. With the move from the TBM to the HondaJet, and the added complexity of EMC2, P3, and multiple aircraft, it made sense to modernize how the money flows. The goal was simple: less invoicing, less back-and-forth, and no one carrying a float.

What Changes

The big shift: Two credit cards instead of one, and moving expenses back to each partner and off of one partner's P&L and accounting team.

Trip Expenses
Each partner's variable trip costs go on their own card. David carries the appropriate card for each mission, FBO, fuel, hotel, food, and ground transport.
Cirrus SR-22T
All Cirrus costs, insurance, annuals, hangar, and maintenance, go directly to Tony. Rich's team reimburses Rich's share.
Maintenance, Insurance & Recurrency
The same card David carries for each partner's trips. Vendors would be given two credit cards and/or two ACH/wire accounts, one for each partner. Maintenance visits, insurance premiums, and recurrency training each billed directly to each partner's account.
Maintenance Reserves
EMC2 and P3 reserves billed monthly, no more end-of-year surprises.
Payment Terms
Net 15 or auto-ACH on fixed monthly invoices.

What Stays the Same

Fixed Costs
Pilot, loan, hangar, amortization continue through Laminar with monthly invoicing.
Cost Split
50/50 on shared costs. Tony's minimum remains 5 flight hours, 5 cycles, and half the calendar rate.
Partnership
Same team, same planes, same pilot. Just a cleaner way to handle the money.
The bottom line: This structure was designed so neither partner would be carrying a balance for the other. Each person's expenses would flow through their own accounts. Shared costs would stay centralized through Laminar with predictable monthly billing. Simpler for everyone, including accounting.
