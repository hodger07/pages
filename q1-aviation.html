If the aircraft flies fewer than 120 hours or 120 cycles in a calendar year, Honda Aircraft and GE Honda issue reconciliation invoices for the shortfall. Monthly billing of these reserves prevents that from becoming a lump-sum surprise at year-end.

How the 5-hour monthly minimum works. Each partner pays for 5 flight hours and 5 cycles every month regardless of whether they fly. Unused hours and cycles carry forward through the year - so if you don't fly in January, that capacity is still available in later months, but the cost is already committed. The only way maintenance costs go beyond the numbers shown above is if a partner exceeds their 60 hours or 60 cycles per year. Beyond that, the only additional costs are fuel and travel expenses for each trip.

Option B — Full Separation of Risk

The current financial structure was built for a smaller, simpler operation. With the move from the TBM to the HondaJet - and the added complexity of EMC2, P3, and multiple aircraft - it makes sense to modernize how the money flows. The goal is simple: less invoicing, less back-and-forth, and no one carrying a float.

What Changes

The big shift: Two credit cards instead of one - and moving expenses back to each partner and off of one partner's P&L and accounting team.

Trip Expenses
Each partner's variable trip costs go on their own card. David carries the appropriate card for each mission - FBO, fuel, hotel, food, ground transport.
Cirrus SR-22T
All Cirrus costs (insurance, annuals, hangar, maintenance) go directly to Tony. Rich's team reimburses Rich's share.
Maintenance, Insurance
& Recurrency
Same card David carries for each partner's trips. Vendors will be given two credit cards and/or two ACH/wire transfer accounts - one for each partner. Maintenance visits, insurance premiums, and recurrency training each billed directly to each partner's account. Whichever method works for the vendor.
Maintenance Reserves
EMC2 and P3 reserves billed monthly - no more end-of-year surprises.
Payment Terms
Net 15 or auto-ACH on fixed monthly invoices.

What Stays the Same

Fixed Costs
Pilot, loan, hangar, amortization - continue through Laminar with monthly invoicing.
Cost Split
50/50 on shared costs. Tony's minimum: 5 flight hours, 5 cycles, half the calendar rate.
Partnership
Same team, same planes, same pilot. Just a cleaner way to handle the money.
The bottom line: This structure is designed so neither partner is carrying a balance for the other. Each person's expenses flow through their own accounts. Shared costs stay centralized through Laminar with predictable monthly billing. It's simpler for everyone - including the accounting team on both sides.
