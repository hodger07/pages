Aviation Partnership Review

A conversation guide — not a collections call

The Conversation

Beat 1 · Open
Acknowledge it. Be direct. No warm-up.
"Tony, I appreciate you making time for this. I'm gonna be direct because that's how we work best — the partnership finances have gotten sideways, and that's on both of us. I want to fix the system so we're not here again, and I want to make sure this still works for you."

30 seconds. Then stop. Don't fill the silence.

Beat 2 · Check In
Ask the real question before the numbers.
"Before we get into the details — is this partnership still working for you at this level?"

Genuine question. Wait for the answer. If Tony's been avoiding because the costs are more than he wants to carry, you need to know now — before building a better system for something he secretly wants to scale back.

Beat 3 · The Fix
Present the restructure as an upgrade, not a correction.
"We went from a $240K/year TBM to a $300K/year jet. The accounting never caught up. Here's what I want to put in place."

The changes:

→ Your trip expenses go on your card. David carries your card for your missions.
→ Cirrus costs flip — you pay directly, my team reimburses my half.
→ EMC² and P3 reserves get billed monthly starting now.
→ Net 15 or auto-ACH — eliminates the check cycle entirely.

"I don't want to be your bank and you don't want to owe me. This fixes both."
Beat 4 · The Number
Name the balance. Ask for a date.
"Right now there's about $57,500 open across four invoices, plus another $18K in maintenance reserves I haven't billed yet. How do you want to handle getting current?"

Don't say "you owe me." Say "there's $57K open." Then ask how he wants to handle it — give him agency on the method, not the whether.

You need a specific date. Not "soon." Not "I'll get to it." A date you can tell your accounting team.

Beat 5 · The Future
Plant the seed — costs are going up.
"I also want you to see where we're headed. The P3 maintenance rates escalate about 15% per tier. By 2029, your monthly floor is closer to $28–29K. I want us both going in with eyes open."

This is respect — giving Tony information to make decisions. It also subtly signals that this partnership is a privilege that gets more expensive, not less.

When Tony Deflects

He will. Here's what to say.

If he changes the subject
Tony pivots to a trip, the planes, anything else.
"Yeah, we can get to that. But I need to finish this first."
If he goes vague
"Yeah man, I know, I'll get that taken care of."
"Good. When? I need a specific date I can tell my accounting team."
If he gets agreeable but non-committal
"That sounds reasonable, let's figure it out."
"Good. So the $57K — can you have that cleared by [date]? And I'll have Kristie send you the new expense setup by Friday."
If he gets emotional / plays the relationship card
"Rich, you know I'm good for it..."
"I know you are. That's not the question. The question is when, and how we set this up so neither of us has to have this conversation again."
If he pushes back on the restructure
"That seems like a lot of changes."
"It's actually simpler. One card per person. Your expenses on yours, mine on mine. Less accounting for everyone."
The one phrase that kills deflection

"I need a specific date I can tell my accounting team."

Close the Call With Two Commitments

Commitment 1
A date for the $57,517.
"So I'll tell Kristie to expect that by [date]. Good?"
Commitment 2
Agreement on the new expense structure.
"And you're good with splitting the cards and flipping the Cirrus expenses? I'll have my team send the details so we're all on the same page."
Do not accept verbal agreement as resolution. End the call with: "I'll have Kristie send you a summary of what we agreed to today. Can you confirm it by end of day tomorrow?" Written confirmation or it didn't happen.
How you know it worked

Whether Tony makes the first payment within 10 days. Not whether he agreed. Not whether it felt good. The check clears or it doesn't.
