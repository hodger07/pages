Tony's share of the operation has grown from roughly $20,000 per month on the TBM to $25,000 per month on the HondaJet. But about half of that increase — $27,000 annually — was already coming when the TBM moved out of warranty and realistic maintenance costs reached $120,000 per year. The HondaJet added $36,000 on top of that, mostly from the EMC² and P3 maintenance programs and an amortization reserve that didn't exist on the TBM.

The loan payment actually went down $820 a month. The cost increase is almost entirely in maintenance and reserves — the structural cost of operating a jet vs. a turboprop.

A Simpler Structure Going Forward

The current financial structure was built for a smaller, simpler operation. With the move from the TBM to the HondaJet — and the added complexity of EMC², P3, and multiple aircraft — it makes sense to modernize how the money flows. The goal is simple: less invoicing, less back-and-forth, and no one carrying a float.

What Changes

Trip Expenses
Each partner's variable trip costs go on their own card. David carries the appropriate card for each mission — FBO, fuel, hotel, food, ground transport.
Cirrus SR-22T
All Cirrus costs (insurance, annuals, hangar, maintenance) go directly to Tony. Rich's team reimburses Rich's share.
Maintenance Reserves
EMC² and P3 reserves billed monthly — no more end-of-year surprises.
Payment Terms
Net 15 or auto-ACH on fixed monthly invoices.

What Stays the Same

Fixed Costs
Pilot, loan, hangar, insurance, amortization, recurrency — continue through Laminar with monthly invoicing.
Cost Split
50/50 on shared costs. Tony's minimum: 5 flight hours, 5 cycles, half the calendar rate.
Partnership
Same team, same planes, same pilot. Just a cleaner way to handle the money.
The bottom line: This structure is designed so neither partner is carrying a balance for the other. Each person's expenses flow through their own accounts. Shared costs stay centralized through Laminar with predictable monthly billing. It's simpler for everyone — including the accounting team on both sides.
