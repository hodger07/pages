Why This Document Exists

Last month I published 21 by 10 — the operating framework. Five functions, twenty-one metrics, cleared by 10 AM, every day.

Frameworks on paper change nothing. So we're building the software that puts teeth behind the framework. The working name is UnifiedOps. It runs alongside Service Titan — it does not replace it. ST keeps doing what ST does well (dispatch, field execution, customer comms, invoicing). UnifiedOps does the parts ST does badly or not at all: the daily DMR rhythm, invoice coaching, manager accountability, exception reporting, and the morning 21 by 10 ritual.

This document is your shot to push back before we build. If something in here is wrong, missing, or going to make your daily life harder instead of easier, I want to hear it now. Once the code is written and the team is using it, every change costs more.

"The list makes the red visible. The team makes the red green. The software is just the list — well-engineered, hard to ignore, and impossible to hide from."

01   What This Is

In one paragraph, what are we building?

UnifiedOps is a web application — used on desktops, iPads, iPhones, and Android — that captures the operational data we need to run the 21 by 10 every day, in real time. It enforces a 15-minute service-level standard from when a tech finishes a job to when that job is invoice-coached and happy-called. It surfaces every red on the 21 by 10 board by 10 AM each morning, with named owners and same-day plans, so nothing rots quietly. And it gives every role — Owner, Manager, Dispatcher, CSR, Controller, Recruiter, Tech — a single screen where their work for the day lives, with the rules baked in so nobody can skip steps or close items prematurely.

One sentence for skeptics: It's the structural enforcement of "today's work today, honest by 10 AM, no quiet reds" — turned into software that doesn't get tired, doesn't take days off, and doesn't let things slip through the cracks.

03   The Daily 21×10 Board

Where the framework becomes a screen.

The board is the single most important screen in the system. Every leadership role starts their morning here.

How it works

  • Five functions, twenty-one metrics — same structure as the framework document, code-locked.
  • Each metric has a per-shop monthly target — your "what green means" homework, captured here. The system computes daily/weekly/MTD pacing against that target.
  • Every metric has a named owner and a named marker — and the marker is never the owner. That's the structural prevention of quiet reds.
  • Three states per metric: 🟢 Green (hit target) · 🟡 Owned (red with named owner and same-day plan) · 🔴 Unowned Red (the violation).
  • By 10 AM local shop time, every unowned red auto-escalates to the Owner. The metric stays visibly flagged.
  • "Cleared" ≠ "Green." Cleared means owned. A board is cleared when zero metrics are unowned red.

04   The 15-Minute Discipline

Today's work today. Measured in minutes.

When a tech notifies the office that he's done with a job (currently via Slack), a 15-minute clock starts. Within that window, both of the following must be true:

  • The invoice has been coached by the Manager
  • The happy call has been completed by the CSR

If either is missing at 15 minutes, an exception fires automatically. At 30 minutes, it escalates to the Owner. The system literally does not let you forget — and it does not let you mark something done when it isn't.

What happens after that

The happy call captures a 1-10 customer score. Anything 7 or below auto-escalates to a Manager for follow-up. Until that escalation is resolved, the call is not considered closed.

There is no "close" button on a job. A call closes only when all of the following are complete: job entered, invoice coached, happy call done, any escalation resolved, any customer complaint resolved. The workflow itself is the close mechanism. You can't sweep it off your plate. The only way it disappears is by doing the work.

Free coaching signal: The system also captures how long the office took to enter the job after the tech Slacked. If a CSR is sitting on a tech's notification for ten minutes, that's measurable — and it shows up as a coaching opportunity for the office, not just for the tech.

05   The Exception Report

What ST doesn't show, but you need to see.

Two kinds of exceptions show up here:

1. Auto-generated by the system

  • SLA breaches (coaching or happy call not done within 15 min)
  • Happy call score ≤ 7 (auto-escalated)
  • Tech chronic late (3+ in 7 days)
  • Tech no-show
  • Customer complaint aged past 4 hours
  • 21×10 metric still unowned red at 10:01 AM
  • Any job not closed by midnight

2. Filed by humans

Anyone in the company can file an exception. Techs especially. If a tech sees an equipment problem, a safety concern, a coworker issue, a customer behavior issue — they file it, it routes to the right person, and it becomes part of the record. Bottom-up signal that ST has no way to capture.

How exceptions die

To close one, you write a resolution note. You don't get a "clear" button. You have to type what action you took. Closed exceptions stay queryable forever — they become the coaching/action history. That kills both the "click-close to clear the count" anti-pattern and gives the Owner an auditable record of how exceptions were actually handled.

06   What We're Not Building

Scope discipline matters. Here's what stays out.
We ARE building
  • The Daily 21×10 Board with pacing math
  • Job entry form (manual, parallel to ST)
  • Invoice coaching workflow with 15-min SLA
  • Happy call queue with 1-10 scoring + escalation
  • Customer complaint logging (we capture what ST swallows)
  • Sick/late call-in capture (currently lost in Slack)
  • Real-time updates everywhere — no refresh button
  • Mobile-responsive (manager can coach from a phone)
  • Exception report with bottom-up tech filing
  • Audit trail on everything that matters
We are NOT building
  • A Service Titan replacement (ever)
  • Dispatch board (ST owns this)
  • Customer SMS / arrival notifications (ST owns this)
  • Invoicing or payment processing (ST owns this)
  • Tech timekeeping / payroll (out of scope)
  • Customer-facing portal
  • Predictive scheduling / AI auto-coaching
  • Multi-tenant franchise hierarchy (V1's mistake)
  • Mobile-native app for techs (web works for now)
  • Manual "close" buttons that bypass workflow

Once UnifiedOps is stable, a future phase will ingest data from Service Titan (read-only) to auto-fill several of the metrics that are currently manual entry. But that's after launch. The MVP runs in parallel to ST with double-entry on a small handful of fields. The discipline of the 21 by 10 is worth more than the cost of a few extra keystrokes per day.

07   Phased Build

What's done, what's next, what's later.

Several pieces are already in place from earlier work. The new phases build on top of what's already running.

  • Phases 1–5 Foundation: auth, division/tech models, job entry, scoring criteria, approval queue, basic dashboard. Already in production-dev with 63 passing tests. Done
  • Phase 6 Budgets + legacy DMR formula port. Partially done; the rest gets absorbed into the Pacing Engine that drives the 21×10 board. Partial
  • Phase A0 Nine-role permission system, audit trail across all entities, user management UI. Foundation that everything else depends on. Next
  • Phase A1–A7 Job entry form revamp (Job Outcome Type, customer fields, mobile responsive). Happy Call workflow. Complaint logging. Day Status capture. Mobile pass across all screens. Next
  • Phase B The Daily 21×10 Board itself. Pacing engine. Owner/marker enforcement. The single most important screen. Next
  • Phase C Active Work Board (cross-role workflow view). Every in-flight job visible until fully closed. Next
  • Phase D Real-time SignalR layer — powers live updates on every queue and board. No refresh button anywhere. Next
  • Future Service Titan ingestion (read-only). Slack auto-detection of "done" notifications. PDF reports. Load testing and full deployment guide. Post-MVP

08   Where You Come In

Honest reds make this business better. So does honest pushback.

These are the questions still open. If your job touches any of them, your answer is worth more than mine. Push back hard — better to argue it out now than to rebuild later.

  • Deputy markers: when the primary marker for a metric is out sick or on vacation, who covers? Auto-fall to a backup role, default to Owner, manual reassignment each morning, or hold the red as unowned until they're back?
  • Same-day plan field: when a Marker sets a metric to Owned (red with plan), what fields are required? Free text only, or also a target resolution date?
  • Owner unavailability: when the Owner is traveling and can't enter the morning's data for the metrics that aren't auto-fed, who covers? Designated backup per shop, or SuperAdmin steps in, or do we just live with missing data that day?
  • Tech personal dashboard: what should a tech actually see on their phone when they log in? Today's jobs, RPH, happy-call average, open coaching items, callbacks — anything missing?
  • The owner/marker matrix: we have all 21 locked, but if you read through it and any assignment is wrong for how your shop actually operates, say so now.
  • Metric thresholds (your homework): from the original 21 by 10 doc — what does "green" actually mean as a number for each of the 21 in your shop? Without those numbers, the pacing engine has nothing to grade against.
  • Anything missing entirely: is there an operational pain you've been carrying that nothing in this brief addresses? Now's the time.
